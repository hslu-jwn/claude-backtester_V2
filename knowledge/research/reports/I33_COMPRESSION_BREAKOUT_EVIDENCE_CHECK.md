# I-33 compression-breakout evidence check

Date: 2026-10-28  
Decision: remain parked; no research record or strategy draft.

## Question and result

The narrow question was whether independent empirical research shows that a pre-existing
price/volatility compression predicts the direction or after-cost profitability of a subsequent
intraday breakout in markets transferable to V2. No direct peer-reviewed study matching that
claim was found in the bounded search. Volatility clustering does not supply a directional entry,
and evidence for opening-range or long-horizon breakouts cannot validate I-33's M1-M5 compression
block.

## Closest evidence

- Holmberg, Lönnbark, and Lundström, *Assessing the profitability of intraday opening range
  breakout strategies*, Finance Research Letters 10 (2013),
  <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2012.09.001>. The paper reports evidence of intraday trending in
  crude-oil futures using an opening-range breakout. It is an opening effect, not a pre-existing
  compression signal, and does not validate a fast CFD squeeze rule outside the opening.
- Magner, Sanhueza, and Lavín, *When is a breakout informative? Market conditions and technical
  signals*, Finance Research Letters (2026),
  <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2026.110774>. Historical S&P 500 constituent breakouts load on
  momentum and have no significant factor-adjusted alpha. Its horizons extend to months, not
  intraday bars, and its conditional result does not establish compression as the predictor.
- Girgin, *The Calm Before More Calm? Daily Bollinger and TTM Squeezes, Volatility Clustering and
  the Leverage Effect in S&P 500 Stocks, 2006 to 2025* (SSRN working paper, 2026),
  <https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7600800>. This is not peer reviewed and the author discloses a
  commercial research-platform interest. Its registered daily event study finds no evidence that
  squeezes precede volatility bursts; its squeeze-breakout strategy loses money and underperforms
  a simpler breakout. It is adverse context, not a direct intraday replication.

## Implication

The existing I-33 sources are discretionary price-action/volume books without a reproducible
statistical sample. The bounded academic search adds no independent directional or net-cost
support, while local breakout families already have adverse/decaying evidence. Do not spend a new
18+ cell family on squeeze definitions, EMA/Keltner variants, block duration, breakout buffers,
and exits. Reconsider only if a direct, reproducible, cost-aware intraday study is found or new
order-flow data creates a genuinely distinct mechanism.
