# Turn-of-the-month equity candidate scan

Date: 2026-11-02  
Decision: **reject as a day-flat candidate; recent U.S. and international findings conflict across
window definitions, and none identifies an allowed interior-session return**.

## Evidence

- John J. McConnell and Wei Xu, “Equity Returns at the Turn of the Month,” *Financial Analysts
  Journal* 64(2), 2008, 49–64, [abstract opened](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v64.n2.11). In
  1926–2005 U.S. data, the last trading day plus first three days averaged 0.15% daily for the
  value-weighted market and appeared in 31 of 35 countries. These are daily close-based returns,
  not an intraday execution test.

- Laura Han, Yufeng Han, and Shirley Tian, “The Disappearing Turn-of-Month Effect,” *Finance
  Research Letters* 71, 2025, 106461,
  [article record opened](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2024.106461). S&P 500 daily returns from
  July 1962–December 2020 show complete disappearance after 2001, which the authors associate with
  lower transaction costs and improved liquidity.

- Nuri Volkan Kayacetin, “Infrequent Rebalancing, Risk Deferral, and Equity Returns at the Turn of
  the Month,” *Journal of International Financial Markets, Institutions and Money* 109, 2026,
  102309, [article record opened](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intfin.2026.102309). Across 30 countries
  in 1994–2023, a broader last-four/first-four-day window averages about 10 bp more per day than
  other days and is strongest in recent U.S. data and high-risk states. That eight-day definition
  and risk conditioning are not the classic fixed four-day U.S. test.

## Admission result

The calendar is causal, but the evidence is multi-day close-to-close and does not locate returns
between 10:30 and 15:30 New York. The modern U.S. four-day null conflicts with a favorable broader
eight-day international definition. Choosing the longer window, semi-year ends, or high-risk
states would select among published variants after seeing disagreement. No opened source reports
a predeclared day-flat index-futures/CFD rule net of executable costs.

Create no turn-of-month strategy, window comparison, risk-state filter, or intraday clock scan.
Reconsider only with independent evidence that predefines an interior-session index-futures return,
uses a modern untouched sample, and resolves costs without relying on overnight or multi-day holds.
