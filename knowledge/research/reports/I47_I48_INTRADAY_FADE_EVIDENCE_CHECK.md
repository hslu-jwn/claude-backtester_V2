# I-47/I-48 intraday-fade evidence check

Date: 2026-10-28  
Decision: park both ideas; no research record or strategy draft.

## Question and result

The bounded question was whether independent empirical research validates either of the precise
Davey-style mechanisms in the frozen backlog: (I-47) a session-specific limit fade placed at an
ATR-stretched average high/low, or (I-48) a next-bar limit beyond a fresh 3--5 bar extreme that
opposes 70--80 bar momentum. No peer-reviewed study matching either rule was found. The closest
literature supports only broader reversal hypotheses, uses materially different event timing, or
is adverse once realistic costs are applied.

## Closest evidence and non-transferability

- Caporale and Plastun, *Daily abnormal price changes and trading strategies in the FOREX*,
  Journal of Economic Studies 48 (2020), <https://doi.org/10.1108/JES-11-2019-0503>. The study
  uses hourly data for five FX pairs from 2008--2018 and finds same-day continuation followed by
  next-day reversal after a two-standard-deviation **daily** move. Its simulation explicitly omits
  spreads, fees, and swaps. This is a next-day abnormal-return event, not either session limit
  entry, and it supplies no after-cost support.
- Neely and Weller, *Intraday Technical Trading in the Foreign Exchange Market*, Journal of
  International Money and Finance 22 (2003),
  <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0261-5606(02)00101-8>. Out-of-sample rules on half-hourly bid/ask data
  for four major USD pairs in 1996 find stable predictable components, but no excess return once
  realistic costs and normal trading hours are imposed. It does not test the two Davey entries;
  it is relevant adverse evidence for transferring generic intraday price-rule predictability.
- Frömmel and Lampaert, *Does frequency matter for intraday technical trading?*, Finance Research
  Letters 18 (2016), <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2016.04.014>. More than 8,000 moving-average
  and RSI rules are tested on ten years of tick RUR/USD data using observed time-varying costs and
  a data-snooping-aware test. Costs erase most profits; the remaining evidence is confined to a
  managed-exchange-rate regime. That market and those rules differ, but the result is adverse to
  assuming a generic price-only intraday edge survives spread.
- Fung, Mok, and Lam, *Intraday price reversals for index futures in the US and Hong Kong*,
  Journal of Banking & Finance 24 (2000),
  <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0378-4266(99)00072-2>. Reversals after large **opening** moves can be
  marginally profitable after costs. Opening effects are excluded from the current research
  scope, and the paper does not validate an overnight-session stretched limit or a 70--80 bar
  momentum-conditioned exhaustion entry.

## Implication

Davey's book case studies remain the only direct support in the local source set. I-47 is highly
sensitive to penetration-based limit fills and same-bar stop/target ordering. I-48 is explicitly
dependent on a few large winners, while its next-bar limit beyond the extreme creates additional
fill-selection risk. Adjacent reversal papers cannot justify selecting their sessions, lookbacks,
ATR multipliers, offsets, or exits, and broad cost-aware FX evidence is adverse. Keep both ideas
parked. Reconsider only if an independent study tests a mechanically close limit-entry rule with
bid/ask-aware fills, out-of-sample validation, and results that survive costs and outlier removal.
