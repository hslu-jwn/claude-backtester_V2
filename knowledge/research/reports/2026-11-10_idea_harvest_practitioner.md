# Practitioner idea harvest — 2026-11-10

Twelve leads, ranked by a qualitative judgment of cost clearance, not evidence of profitability. These are research proposals, not approved trading specs or backtests. All URLs below were opened. The original source claim is separated from the concrete proposed adaptation; none of the added stops, CFD substitutions, costs or trade frequencies is validated by the source. PUBLISHED means the underlying effect was published; PRACTITIONER means a practitioner described a rule; SPECULATIVE means the implementation is substantially our own.

**Common simulation conventions.** Daily bars end 17:00 America/New_York; use the first M1 open after a completed signal for entries and signal exits; never fill on the signal close. ATR(14) is Wilder daily true range using completed bars. One position per instrument, no pyramiding; stop orders activated after entry, fills and gaps use available M1 prices and conservative engine rules. Time exits occur at first available M1 open after the stated bar close. For pairs, enter both legs at the first common M1 open, equal initial dollar notionals, and close both if either leg hits its own fixed 2 ATR stop; synchronize valid prices and count both legs, both spreads, slippage and swap. Missing signal or execution data: skip; no future candles. These are explicit research assumptions to be reviewed in a formal spec, not interpretations of existing trading rules.

**Scale and uncertainty.** Ranges below are *unmeasured orders of magnitude*, not expected returns: gross move means absolute entry-to-exit price movement, not signed alpha or average profit. A 50 bp move can have zero expected edge. FX major round trip 1–2 bp; US500 roughly 1–2 bp; metals, energy, crosses and crypto require measured symbol/time-specific spreads, slippage, swap/funding and gaps. Pair costs are sums of both legs, normalized to the same initial leg notional. Cost figures exclude uncertain rollover/swap and volatile-market slippage. Annual trade counts per instrument or pair are crude hypotheses, not observations; estimate them on development data before prioritizing. Longer holds and wider stops do not themselves create an edge. Development ends 2021-09-13; no test-period run without the user's go. Account for all 12 ideas and any variants as a research family, including losers.

## 1. WTI–Brent relative-value spread — SPECULATIVE

Source: [Quantpedia, Trading WTI/Brent Spread](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/trading-wti-brent-spread), a futures spread idea; the CFD rules below are an adaptation, not its published test. Two oils share global demand but regional supply, transport constraints and contract rolls can cause persistent divergence. **Rules:** USOIL/UKOIL, daily. At each close calculate z-score of log(USOIL/UKOIL) against the preceding 60 daily observations, excluding today's observation from mean and standard deviation. If z > 2, short USOIL and long UKOIL; if z < -2, reverse. Exit both when |z| <= 0.5, after 20 trading days, or on a leg stop under common conventions; no new trade until flat. **Claim:** source reports a historical futures spread backtest, not this rule's CFD return. **Scale:** 3–12 paired trades/year; speculative gross spread convergence 50–300 bp against perhaps 10–40+ bp combined execution, plus two swaps. **Caveat:** check actual CFD underlying/roll methodology, negative or zero prices (skip log signal) and 2020 dislocation; two-leg engine and synchronized data required.

## 2. Commodity tail asymmetry — PUBLISHED effect, SPECULATIVE adaptation

Source: [Quantpedia, return asymmetry](https://vvv.quantpedia.com/strategies/return-asymmetry-effect-in-commodity-futures). Source reports 4.36% annualized, 1991–2021, 22 futures and seven legs each side, costs unreported; selected breadth may overfit. Lottery demand may depress assets with more positive tail days. **Rules:** daily XAUUSD, XAGUSD, USOIL, NATGAS; exclude UKOIL to avoid duplicate oil exposure. On first available trading day of each month, calculate for each the last 260 completed daily returns, their sample mean and standard deviation; IE = count above mean + 2 SD minus count below mean - 2 SD. Long lowest IE, short highest; ties broken in listed order. Close at next monthly rebalance or each leg's 2 ATR stop; replace only next month. **Scale:** up to 24 individual entries/year combined; absolute monthly moves 100–800 bp versus potentially 10–60+ bp combined on notionals, plus swap. Small, correlated universe, stop and CFD financing make this a different experiment; current portfolio engine may need multi-leg support.

## 3. Commodity skewness sort — PUBLISHED effect, SPECULATIVE adaptation

Source: [Quantpedia, skewness effect](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/skewness-effect-in-commodities). Its 22-futures 1990–2022 portfolio reports 9.51% annualized for 3/3 positions, not our four-CFD basket. Investors may overpay for lottery-like positive skew and demand compensation for negative tail risk. **Rules:** same four assets and execution as idea 2; on first available trading day of month compute sample third standardized moment of previous 252 daily returns, long lowest and short highest, alphabetical tie break, 2 ATR leg stops, close/rebalance monthly. **Scale:** up to 24 legs/year, 100–800 bp absolute monthly move vs 10–60+ bp plus swap. Related to #2 but different statistic and source; measure correlation and do not count as independent confirmation. Four assets, parameter selection, large tail losses and financing are major weaknesses.

## 4. Oil-led equity risk switch — SPECULATIVE

Source: [Quantpedia, commodity versus gold equity timing](https://quantpedia.com/avoid-equity-bear-markets-with-a-market-timing-strategy-part-2/), which discusses gold/oil predictive research and cautions commodity timing can suffer large drawdowns. **Rules:** US500, USOIL, XAUUSD daily; on first trading day each month compare completed 63-day total price returns in USOIL and gold. If oil return >= gold return, buy US500; otherwise hold cash. 2 ATR fixed US500 stop; exit at next monthly decision; after stop stay flat until next decision. **Mechanism:** industrial demand relative to defensive gold might proxy risk appetite. **Claim:** no claim for this exact oil/gold rule or CFD; source's findings concern different constructions. **Scale:** 2–12 trades/year, 100–500 bp absolute index moves vs ~1–2 bp plus monthly swap. Compare against buy-and-hold and an always-long monthly control; the source and adaptation are vulnerable to data mining and changing oil supply shocks.

## 5. Weekly cross-market reversal — PUBLISHED effect, SPECULATIVE adaptation

Source: [Quantpedia, short-term reversal with futures](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/short-term-reversal-with-futures). Source reports 0.57% weekly long-short, 1983–2000, with futures volume and open-interest filters; neither filter is available as equivalent reliable CFD volume, so this is not a replication. **Rules:** US500, NAS100, GER40, UK100, JP225, daily; every Wednesday 17:00 New York rank prior five completed trading-day close returns; long lowest, short highest, equal dollars, ties in listed order. Each leg stop 2 ATR, exit next Wednesday; require all five synchronized eligible histories. **Mechanism:** cross-market overreaction and liquidity provision. **Scale:** up to 104 leg entries/year pooled, 50–300 bp absolute leg moves vs ~2–10+ bp combined plus swap. Index histories/holidays and correlated losers matter; no tick-volume substitution for centralized futures volume.

## 6. EURUSD long-horizon value reversion — SPECULATIVE

Source: [Quantpedia, FX mean reversion](https://vvv.quantpedia.com/how-to-build-mean-reversion-strategies-in-currencies/), which studies monthly deviation-based FX-futures portfolios; this single spot CFD proxy has no comparable claim. **Rules:** EURUSD daily; on first trading day each month compare close with 252-day mean of closes; z-score uses preceding 252 closes excluding signal close. Long if z < -2, short if z > 2; close at next month decision if |z| < 0.5, otherwise hold for at most 3 months; 3 ATR fixed stop, no additional entries while open. **Mechanism:** extreme exchange-rate deviations may overshoot longer-run value. **Scale:** 0–6 entries/year, 100–500 bp absolute moves vs 1–2 bp plus potentially substantial multi-month swap. A moving average is not fundamental fair value; 2015–2021 offers few independent cycles. No claim of profit for this proxy.

## 7. Double seven index pullback — PRACTITIONER

Source: [Alvarez, Double 7s](https://alvarezquanttrading.com/blog/double-7s-strategy/). Source buys a 7-day low above 200-day average and exits a 7-day high, but warns that the effect may have disappeared. **Rules:** US500 daily; if close > SMA200 and close <= minimum of current and preceding six closes, enter long next M1 open; exit after close >= maximum of current and preceding six closes, after 15 trading days, or fixed 2 ATR stop. **Mechanism:** temporary selling pressure reverses within a positive long-term regime. **Scale:** 5–25 trades/year; 30–200 bp absolute moves vs 1–2 bp plus swap. Source's close fill differs, and this 7-day-low strategy shares broad mean-reversion risk with the already-screened RSI(2) family; it is not an RSI(2) rerun and incremental evidence is required.

## 8. Three descending lows pullback — PRACTITIONER

Source: [Alvarez / Radge pullback](https://alvarezquanttrading.com/blog/simple-ideas-for-a-mean-reversion-strategy-with-good-results/), originally individual stocks. **Rules:** NAS100 daily; close above SMA100 and below SMA5, with today's low < yesterday's low < low two days ago; long next M1 open. Exit after first daily close above previous close, 10 trading days, or 2 ATR stop. **Mechanism:** a serial intratrend selloff may overshoot. **Claim:** source describes a stock portfolio with different eligibility and execution; no transferable CFD claim. **Scale:** 5–30 trades/year; 40–250 bp absolute moves vs index spread/slippage and swap. Related to #7, but distinct entry/exit and market; count jointly with other pullback trials, and evaluate overlap with the failed RSI(2) screen.

## 9. Volatility-normalized extreme return pullback — SPECULATIVE

Source: [Alvarez, recent returns](https://alvarezquanttrading.com/blog/using-recent-returns-for-mean-reversion/), which ranks two-day returns and uses an ATR limit on stocks with an RSI exit. **Rules:** US500 daily, close above SMA100; calculate two-day close return and its percentile relative to prior 252 completed two-day returns, excluding today; setup percentile < 5%. Place a one-day buy limit 0.5 ATR(10) below setup close beginning next M1 open, cancel at next daily close. After fill exit at first daily close above SMA5, at 10 days, or 2 ATR(14) stop. **Mechanism:** rare, locally extreme selloffs may attract liquidity provision; the limit seeks a larger move, but may select falling knives. **Claim:** no source result for this index/exit/stop. **Scale:** 2–15 fills/year, 50–250 bp absolute move vs ~1–2 bp plus slippage and swap; limit fills need gap-aware handling. Related to #7–8; not an RSI(2) signal, though economically correlated.

## 10. Daily close-location reversal — SPECULATIVE

Source: [Alvarez, mean-reversion indicators](https://alvarezquanttrading.com/blog/the-abcs-of-creating-a-mean-reversion-strategy-part1/) discusses a close's position in a multi-day range as a pullback measure. **Rules:** US500 daily, close above SMA200; use current and prior four complete days, compute (close - 5-day lowest low)/(5-day highest high - 5-day lowest low); if < 0.10 buy next M1 open; skip zero range. Exit at first close-location > 0.75, after 10 trading days, or 2 ATR stop. **Mechanism:** a close near a multi-day extreme can mark short-term selling exhaustion. **Claim:** no reported result for this exact construction. **Scale:** 5–30/year; 30–180 bp absolute movement against ~1–2 bp plus swap. It is another index pullback and should be judged as part of that family, not independent validation.

## 11. Bitcoin drawdown rebound — SPECULATIVE

Source: [Quantpedia, Bitcoin trend and mean reversion](https://quantpedia.com/trend-following-and-mean-reversion-in-bitcoin/?a=6080), reports local-minimum reversal can have very large drawdowns. **Rules:** Binance BTC perpetual, UTC daily bars; if today's close is at least 20% below the maximum of the preceding 30 closes, and yesterday was less than 20% below its corresponding preceding 30-close maximum, buy at next M1 open. Exit on first close above SMA10, after 10 daily bars, or fixed 2 ATR stop; one trade per threshold crossing. Include actual timestamped funding payments and futures fees; skip missing funding. **Mechanism:** liquidation-driven overshoot may reverse, but can continue during deleveraging. **Claim:** no claim for this precise threshold/stop. **Scale:** 1–10/year, 300–2000 bp absolute movement vs measured fees/spread/slippage and funding, potentially tens of bp or far more in stress. 20% is a proposed starting rule, not optimized; stop gap and 24/7 availability matter. Distinct from tested H1 shock and RSI(2), but crypto crash regimes could dominate.

## 12. Gold–silver ratio spread — SPECULATIVE

Source: [Robot Wealth, practical pairs](https://robotwealth.com/practical-pairs-trading/) explains hedge ratios and warns that testing many pairs mines noise; it does not establish this specific pair or profitability. **Rules:** XAUUSD/XAGUSD daily. Compute z-score of log(XAGUSD/XAUUSD) relative to prior 90 daily log ratios; z > 2: short silver, long gold; z < -2: long silver, short gold. Equal initial dollar notionals, 2 ATR individual stops; close both at |z| <= 0.5 or after 20 trading days. **Mechanism:** precious metals share monetary drivers, but silver's industrial demand and higher volatility can produce divergent moves. **Scale:** 3–15 paired trades/year, 100–500 bp absolute spread movement vs possibly 10–50+ bp total execution and two swaps. Equal dollar hedges are intentionally simple, not proof of cointegration; structural divergence and asymmetric leg risk are serious.

## Research order and limits

Start with #1, #2, #3, #4 and #5, subject to checking availability, CFD contract continuity and all-in costs first. Ranking is qualitative: #1 has a natural economic linkage and possible large spread moves; #2–3 have published futures cross-sectional evidence but a drastically smaller available universe; #4 is a long-horizon intermarket conditional exposure rather than a tiny intraday signal; #5 has published weekly reversal evidence but loses the original volume/open-interest filters. The ranking is not a prediction and does not validate a trading rule. #7–10 share a pullback family and the prior RSI(2) rejection is adverse evidence; #11 has large gross movement but severe crash, funding and slippage risk. No source performance above is a result for our exact stop-defined CFD implementations. Before implementation, specify calendar/holiday bars, fills, cost and swap history, paired exposure accounting, controls and gates; no one-time test without the user's authorization.
