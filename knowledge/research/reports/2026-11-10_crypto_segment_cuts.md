# BTCUSD and ETHUSD development segment cuts - 2026-11-10

## Scope and method

Read the current MT5 cache through `DEFAULT_SOURCES` and call `backtester.tools.research.segments` for 2017-04-01 through 2021-09-13 inclusive. This is the development interval only; BTCUSD starts 2017-04-06 and ETHUSD starts 2018-01-10. Each row is one boundary between consecutive returned segments. The range is the inclusive calendar gap between the last and first cached day in those segments. `sources.covered_days` adds the existing `weekly_closures` rule; it infers missing Saturdays only in years with zero Saturday files, between that year's first and last physical files. An inferred Saturday within a longer hole is included in the range and missing-minute count, even though it is already covered by that rule. Segmentation is based on day-file existence, including empty files; it does not assess intraday completeness.

Minutes are 1,440 times the number of absent day files in the hole, not a claim that the broker should have traded those minutes. Boundary columns give distinct valid UTC minute timestamps out of 1,440 and the first-last timestamp for the adjacent cached days. Thus 1,440/1,440 is a complete calendar day; a lower count may be a normal partial trading day, sparse ticks or missing data. These figures do not count gaps inside boundary days as part of the hole. Classification is inference from cache patterns, not proof of broker schedules.

2018 contains one Saturday file for each symbol (2018-03-24); 2021 has Saturday files beginning 2021-09-18, after the development cutoff. Consequently the year-wide rule rejects all inferred Saturdays in both years. In 2018 many April-December Friday/Sunday boundaries show regular early close/reopen; early 2018 has many nearly full Sunday files, so the schedule is less certain. In 2021 the repeated Saturday holes continue through September 11, just before Saturday files begin; adjacent days often have partial or fragmented coverage. The fact that both symbols share a hole is consistent with a common broker schedule or a common acquisition outage, and cannot distinguish them alone.

## Every cut

Ranges and boundary times are UTC. `Uncovered days` counts missing dates that actually violate the current closure rule. A range is categorized once, even when it includes both a likely closure and other missing days. Weekdays name the first and last date.

## Smallest defensible handling proposal

Keep the existing strict default and add only broker-confirmed, symbol- and date-specific closure intervals to covered days, with no fabricated candles; use the same coverage logic for segmentation and adapter validation. A compact verified schedule of Saturday closure periods could replace individual dates, but it must preserve actual Saturday files and the switch to Saturday trading. The present data suggests a Saturday regime before 2021-09-18, but the 2018-03-24 exception and near-full Sundays mean a blanket all-Saturdays rule would silently hide potential missing trading days. Verify historical broker trading hours and that exceptional file first.

Do not infer closure for an entire multi-day hole merely because it contains Saturday, nor automatically classify Christmas/New Year as closed: verify the exact dates from the broker-s historical schedule. Leave every unverified weekday, holiday and long outage as a cut. For example, 2019-02-22-2019-03-09, 2020-03-07-2020-03-28, 2020-10-24-2020-10-31 and 2021-03-13-2021-03-27 contain substantial weekday loss; 2020-11-30 and 2021-05-31 are standalone Monday gaps. Even after a confirmed Saturday calendar, such ranges retain cuts where other dates remain missing. Calendar rules must not depend on future candle prices, synthesize prices, or fill holes; changing coverage requires tests for transitions, partial years, cached Saturday exceptions and genuine gaps. No proposed cut-reduction count is asserted without a confirmed calendar.
