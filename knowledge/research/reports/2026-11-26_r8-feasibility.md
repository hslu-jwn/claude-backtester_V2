# R8 return-blind feasibility audits (2026-11-26, Sonnet worker)

No price return was computed or read. Samples are in `data/cache/r8/` (3 MB). "Quote" = verbatim, from the opened source. UNVERIFIED = not opened or only seen in a search snippet.

## 1. SEC Rule 201 trigger archives

- **Nasdaq (works, anonymous HTTP).** `https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/dynamic/symdir/shorthalts/shorthalts{YYYYMMDD}.txt`. CSV: `Symbol,Security Name,Market Category,Trigger Time` (to the second). Downloaded 11 days, 2016-02-16 to 2026-01-05, every one returned 200. A non-trading day (2016-02-15) gave 302, so there is no file for holidays. Each file holds the day's triggers plus the prior day's carryover (dedupe by the date in Trigger Time). Page quote: "Download" link for `shorthalts20261125.txt` on `trader.aspx?id=ShortSaleCircuitBreaker`.
- **NYSE group (works, anonymous).** I did not find this in the SEC or review text. It is `https://ftp.nyse.com/NYSEGroupSSRCircuitBreakers/NYSEGroupSSRCircuitBreakers_{YYYY}/..._{YYYYMM}/NYSEGroupSSRCircuitBreakers{YYYYMMDD}.xls`. Format: .xls, sheet `SSR_data`, columns Trigger Date, Issue Symbol, Issue Name, Primary Exchange (NYSE, MKT, ARCA/P, Z), SSR State, Trigger Time, Anticipated Deactivation Date. One file per day, same-day triggers only. I enumerated the month directories for 2016-2025 and counted 250-253 files a year, with no empty month. Sampled 6 days (2017-2025), all downloadable. 2020-03-16 had 3,814 rows.
- **Cboe (works, anonymous).** `https://www-api.cboe.com/us/equities/market_statistics/short_sale_circuit_breakers/downloads/BatsCircuitBreakers{YEAR}.csv`. I got 2015, 2016, 2019, 2023 and 2026. Columns: Primary Listing Exchange, Symbol, Trigger/End/Rescinded Date and Time. Old years are tiny (9, 25, 35, 68 rows), so Cboe holds only BZX-listed names. 2026 has 10,958 rows, which looks like a bulk change. Quote: "Download 2026 as CSV" (page lists only 2026; the older years work by URL guessing).
- **Counts (same-day trigger rows).** Nasdaq: 53 to 177 on ordinary days, 232 to 1,240 on stress days. Of these, Global Select is 18 to 58 on ordinary days; the rest is Capital Market. NYSE group: 13 to 37 on ordinary days in 2017-2019, 258 to 463 in 2023-2024. These include ETPs, warrants and micro caps, so most rows are not liquid names.
- **Independent dates among liquid names (ESTIMATE, UNVERIFIED).** I could not rank by market cap without the share universe. If the top-1,000 take about 5-10 % of the rows, that gives about 2-15 names a day and well over 100 trigger dates a year. Stage B's -10.5 to -10.0 % bin keeps a minority of those, so treat 50-100 dates a year as the plausible floor. Note the 2020 and 2024-08 clustering.
- Open items for the census: Nasdaq file retention before 2016 is not tested; point-in-time symbol mapping; ETP and warrant filtering; LULD overlap.
- **Verdict: GO to the return-blind census card.** Archives are complete and anonymous for 2016-2025 (NYSE 250+ files a year). The power estimate rests on a proxy.

## 2. CRSP fast-track IPO inclusion

- **Rule (partly public).** Source: Murray and Sammon, RAPS 16(2), https://academic.oup.com/raps/article/16/2/163/8416725. I read it through the fetch tool's summary, not the full text. Fast-Track depends on market cap at the first-day close. Before March 2017 the mid-cap threshold applied, after that the small-cap threshold. Free float must be at least 10 % of shares outstanding, the firm must be primarily US, and the security type must be eligible. Inclusion is at the fifth trading day's closing auction (T+4). Quote: "only two stocks were added via Fast-Track between October 2014 and February 2017".
- **Events a year.** 262 additions from March 2017 to December 2022, about 45 a year (my calculation from the period total; the paper gives no year split). The breakpoint is in the top of the small-cap band, about $2.4 bn in Dec 2024 and about $3.0 bn in June 2026 (search snippet, UNVERIFIED).
- **Public inputs.** CRSP publishes quarterly breakpoints (breakpoints chart, now behind a JS-only Morningstar site, so I could not read the history). Day-one close and shares come from the prospectus. The methodology PDF named in the register (crsp.org, Jan 2025) returns 404, and the Morningstar rulebook is a JS page I could not read. So the exact text of the rule, including buffers and security-type filters, is UNVERIFIED.
- **Rule change.** Secondary sources (Schwab, Morningstar, search snippets, UNVERIFIED) say CRSP added an absolute float-value test in April 2026 ("10% FSO or roughly $3.3 billion in float-adjusted market capitalization"). That is a regime break for any 2026 sample, but not for 2017-2025.
- **Risk.** The first-day float used by CRSP (FSO) may differ from the prospectus float; the audit must test agreement on a sample, which I could not do here.
- **Verdict: GO to a return-blind eligibility card (cautious).** About 45 events a year clears the 10 a year bar. Whether public data reproduces CRSP's decision is not proven. The first card must be a reproduction test on the published breakpoints.

## 3. Non-overnight follow-on offerings

- **Counts.** Total US follow-ons: 617 in 2018 and 552 in 2019, with overnight/bought deals about 36 % of 2019 (a search snippet from a law-firm and S&P source, UNVERIFIED). SEC's FRO statistics (https://www.sec.gov/data-research/statistics-data-visualizations/follow-registered-offerings-fros) give 1,085 total FROs and 775 US issuers in 2025, with a median of $14.0 m. This is aggregate only (from SDC), with no event-level rows, and its download is described as 2000Q1-2026Q2.
- **Rough marketed count (ESTIMATE).** About 550 follow-ons a year, times about 64 % non-overnight, gives about 350. After removing small names, financials and utilities, secondaries, units and ATMs, and keeping top-1,000 liquid names, I estimate 50-100 a year. This is thin against the 50 a year stop and cannot be firmed up without the event list.
- **Clock.** The free EDGAR submissions API and nightly bulk files give 424B5 and FWP filings, but I could not confirm an `acceptanceDateTime` field on the page (the page names no form types). The EDGAR page says history is "at least one year's of filing or to 1,000". The clock problem: the preliminary 424B5 time gives the announcement, but the final 424B5 is filed after pricing (often the next morning). The true pricing time is in the press release (wire timestamps), which has no free bulk source I found.
- **Verdict: PARK on data.** No free reproducible pricing clock. Power is borderline at about 50-100 a year. Reopen only if someone builds a 424B5 plus wire-release timestamp pipeline.

## 4. Hyperliquid HLP backstop transfers

- **Mechanism is protocol-defined.** Quote: "Positions that are below 2/3 of the maintenance margin can be taken over by the liquidator vault." (docs, trading/liquidations). Fills carry an optional `liquidation` object with `method` ("market" or "backstop"), `liquidatedUser`, `markPx` (Dwellir research page). The standalone Liquidator vault is retired and its function sits inside HLP (search snippet, UNVERIFIED).
- **Free history does not exist.** The official archive `hl-mainnet-node-data` holds `node_fills_by_block` and `node_fills`, but it is requester-pays. Quote: "the requester of the data must pay for transfer costs." That needs an AWS account and payment, so I did not touch it. Bitquery's API retains about 30 days. Quote: "Liquidation history on the API goes back about 30 days". It also says: "Backstop liquidations are rare, so for most wallets this returns nothing." Dune's Hyperliquid tables are enterprise or trial only and start January 2025. The public info API only returns recent trades (Allium note, UNVERIFIED).
- **Cluster count.** None available. Hyperliquid launched in 2023, so the window is at most about 3 years, and "rare" argues against 50 independent non-intervention clusters.
- **Verdict: PARK on data** (also likely on power).

## 5. Rights issues (2,583-event dataset)

- **Location.** https://zenodo.org/records/18054892 (CC BY 4.0, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.18054892), files `Verdu_Carchano_Ruiz_2025_Data.csv` (370 kB) and a description .xlsx. The web download path returned 403, but the API route (`/api/records/18054892/files/.../content`) worked.
- **Content (metadata only; I did not read the ARR/ARB return columns).** 4,015 rows (not 2,583; probably three pricing periods: PER1 601, PER2 1,677, PER3 1,737). Columns: IDE (anonymous id), Region, Country, PER, ARR, ARB, OUT, DIL, IDX, ISC, CAP, plus industry dummies. There is no date, ticker, exchange, right symbol, ratio, deadline or quote. Rows by country: SWE 974, HKG 474, MYS 350, AUS 313, FRA 301, SGP 296, ITA 150, BRA 146, LKA 104, CAN 91, ESP 85, PAK 79, GBR 73, NOR 66, IND 57, FIN 56, BEL 55, GRC 51, DEU 46, EGY 44, USA 39 (the review said 18 US cases; the CSV has 39 rows, possibly periods), then smaller ones.
- **Consequence.** Post-2015 filtering and quote-synchronization cannot be done from this file at all. It cannot be joined to a venue's quotes without rebuilding every event from exchange notices (Nasdaq Stockholm, Borsa Italiana, Euronext, HKEX, ASX). Whether Interactive Brokers shows a given right as tradable is UNVERIFIED: its help text says only "when rights are listed for trading, customers who are short them can try to close", and some rights are not listed.
- **Executable cases.** Not countable. The countries a Swiss IBKR account probably reaches (SWE, HKG, AUS, FRA, SGP, ITA, CAN, ESP, GBR, NOR, FIN, BEL, DEU) hold about 2,700 of the rows, but with no dates and no quote evidence the executable count is 0 verified.
- **Verdict: PARK on data.**
