# Volman, "Forex Price Action Scalping" (2011) - notes part 1 (lines 1-6300)

Range covered: Preface, Section 1 (Ch 1-5, pp. 3-30), Section 2 start: Ch 6 The Setups (p33), Ch 7 Double Doji Break (p39), Ch 8 First Break (p61), Ch 9 Second Break (p79), Ch 10 Block Break (p109), Ch 11 Range Break (p137) up to Fig 11.9 (about p171). NOT covered here (second half): rest of Ch 11 (Fig 11.10 on), Ch 12 Inside Range Break (p175), Ch 13 Advanced Range Break (p209), Ch 14 Tipping Point Technique = the exit/scratch rules (p241), Ch 15 Unfavorable Conditions (p283), Ch 16 Trade Volume (p309), Ch 17. The exact scratch (exit) rules and the final stop sizing live in Ch 14, so the "exit" parts below are only what is hinted in Ch 4-11.

## Overview
- Pure discretionary price-action scalping of EURUSD on ONE chart: a 70-tick bar chart plus a 20 EMA. No other indicator, no higher-timeframe context ("about 1.5 to 2 hours" of bars on screen is enough).
- Seven setups, all revolving around the 20 EMA and "signal bar -> entry bar" breaks: DD (double doji), FB (first break), SB (second break), BB (block break), RB (range break), IRB, ARB. This part covers the first five.
- Fixed bracket: 10 pip target (non-adjustable limit order), 10 pip max stop (adjustable only toward the target, used to scratch). Market order entry only, 1 pip spread assumed. Only one position at a time, risk 1-2 percent per trade.
- Philosophy: trade probability, not outcome; take every valid setup, skip when there is "chart resistance" or unfavourable conditions; "double pressure" (counter-trend traders forced to exit = with-trend orders) is the claimed edge.
- Everything is described qualitatively with annotated charts. There is NO backtest, NO hit-rate table. The only quantified claims: avg winner 10 pip, avg loser about 6 pip (because of scratches), so target needs to be hit in only about 40 percent of trades to profit (p. 76). Author himself warns (p. 41-42) that results of such a method in a backtest are not reproducible live because of the human hand.
- For us: the setups are codeable only after heavy simplification; the discretionary filters (chart resistance, "quality" of the pullback, squeeze, tease/false break) are where the supposed edge lives and are the hard part.

## Chapter notes

### Preface and Ch 1 - Trading Currencies (pp. V-6)
- Chosen pair EURUSD: avg daily range about 150 pip, spread must be <= 1 pip round turn (retail no-commission) or about 0.5 pip + commission. If cost > 1 pip "the line between winning and losing is crossed".
- Broker risks: spread mark-up in news, requotes. He says do not trade when spread widens (up to 10 pip in news).
- Principles are said to transfer to other markets with similar volatility and cost.

### Ch 2 - The Tick Chart (pp. 7-11)
- 70-tick bars: a new bar every 70 transactions (not volume). "Sometimes resembles a 30-second chart". Thousands of bars per day. Tick count varies with data vendor; advice: choose the tick number so that the chart looks like a ~30-second chart in the ACTIVE sessions (65 or 75 would not change patterns, other vendors might need 40 or 100+).
- Typical bar height: calm market 2-4 pip; trend bars a few pip taller.
- Asian session (about 02:00-10:00 in his chart time, apparently GMT+1/+2 server time) prints far fewer bars per hour. News: 3 minutes printed as many bars as the previous 40 minutes (13x faster).
- Chart prices must be rounded to whole pips so that equal highs/lows can be seen (pipette precision ruins "equal extremes"). Use a separate charting package whose price matches the platform bid/ask within a pip.
- Screen shows about 1.5-2 hours = "a couple of hundred bars".

### Ch 3 - Scalping as a Business (pp. 13-17); Ch 5 - Probability Principle (pp. 27-30)
- Mostly psychology: treat trading as a business, accept losses, think in probabilities, edge only shows over many trades (dice example 7:5). Edge = exploiting other traders' mistakes.
- No predicting direction; stay neutral; "a clever scalper is an observer".

### Ch 4 - Target, Stop and Orders (pp. 19-25)
- Target: fixed 10 pip limit order. Stop: 10 pip (minus spread on some platforms), bracket (OCO) set automatically on entry. Stop can be moved ONLY toward the target (to exit a failing trade early = scratch, "tipping point" technique, Ch 14).
- Entry: market order at the moment the break level trades; no limit orders (missed fills).
- Scalping 20-40 pip out of a 60 pip swing = several re-entries, not wider targets. Changing the target to 12 is explicitly rejected with math (Ch 11, p. 155): extra 2 pip must succeed 6 times per failure just to break even.
- Do not look at P/L during trade.

### Ch 6 - The Setups (pp. 33-38)
- The 20 EMA: slope up = look for longs, down = shorts, flat and price crossing back and forth = be selective (indecisive). Any length 15-25 works; fast (<15) gets pierced constantly, slow (>30) lags / fewer setups. Guide, not a law; EMA is NOT support/resistance, just where pullbacks tend to stall.
- Classification: DD, FB, SB are with-trend pullback setups. BB works anywhere (trend, range, top/bottom, with or counter trend). RB, IRB, ARB are for sideways/topping/bottoming markets.
- Terminology: signal bar = the bar whose extreme is the trigger level; entry bar = the first bar that trades beyond it.
- "Base conditions": 1) overall pressure (higher lows vs lower highs) points in trade direction; 2) path to target free of "chart resistance" (clusters of bars, round numbers, earlier highs/lows within ~10 pip); 3) not an unfavourable condition (Ch 15, second half).

### Ch 7 - Double Doji Break, DD (pp. 39-60)
- Pattern: end of a pullback in a visible trend; two or more neighbouring dojis (or small bars, "usually no more than 3 pip in length") in the 20 EMA zone. Pullback should retrace about 40-60 percent of the last swing, ideally diagonal and one-directional (not a horizontal block).
- Trend quality: majority of bars close in trend direction, bars a few pip taller than average; pullback bars mostly the opposite colour. A new-born trend breaking out of a range also counts.
- Signal bar = the bar of the doji group with the extreme on the trend side (highest high for longs / lowest low for shorts). Best if the dojis have EQUAL trend-side extremes ("signal line"); acceptable if 1 pip apart; 2 pip apart only in strong trend; more than that: skip (weak trend).
- Trigger: market order the moment price trades exactly 1 pip beyond the signal bar's extreme (long: signal high + 1 pip). Watch tick counter because the entry bar can gap by 1 pip on its first tick.
- Stop/target: bracket 10/10 (details of stop at the setup's other side come in Ch 8: signal bar plus 2 pip plus 1 pip spread must fit inside 10).
- Three ways a pullback can meet the EMA (p. 48-49): (1) dojis barely touch the EMA - tradable in a very clear trend, may be early; (2) dojis pierce EMA but close back below/above it - best version; (3) dojis pierce and are not pushed back - riskier, only in very strong trend and if the break comes soon after the EMA is retaken; if bars start resting on the EMA and it flattens, wait for SB/BB.
- Skip rules: (a) chart resistance in the path to 10 pip (cluster of bars, round number zone 50/00 directly ahead, e.g. 1.27 above entry); (b) pullback is a horizontal block instead of a diagonal move; (c) pullback retraces ALL of the prior with-trend move or has taller bars than the trend (countertrend strength); (d) DD bars are NOT compressed compared with the bars before (biggest bars around); (e) a higher low in the pullback / double bottom visible -> possible reversal; (f) very slow subdued market; (g) setup stands tall (cost of a scratch grows); (h) entry already almost at the low of the pullback.
- "Trend equals trend" rule of thumb: the follow-up move often mimics the strength of the pre-pullback move.
- Test-the-breakout-zone: a DD in the EMA that is also a retest of a broken horizontal level is a plus.
- Round numbers: 50-level and 00-level (half and full "cent") zones attract price ("vacuum effect"): thin trading in front of them, so for DD (a tiny 2-bar buildup) do not trade straight into them; other setups (BB) cope better. In a proven trend they are breached easily.
- Do not front-run (enter before the break) - gain is about 1 pip, cost is no-trade losses.

### Ch 8 - First Break, FB (pp. 61-77)
- Rarely traded; he skips most FBs in favour of SB. Three conditions all required: (1) trend = a violent one-directional surge, bars longer than earlier ones, many bars closing on their extremes, ideally shooting out of a sideways consolidation (often news driven); (2) pullback is straight, one-directional (bars mostly opposite colour, no bar in the pullback is broken in the trend direction before the signal bar), retraces about 40-60 percent (up to about 70 percent acceptable; deeper reduces odds), reaches the EMA "area" (EMA may lag a spike - guide not barrier); the combination looks like a "fishhook"; (3) it is the FIRST pullback of the new trend (a move out of a range counts as new).
- Signal bar: any bar in the pullback that gets broken in trend direction (the first such bar). Nice if it is a doji closing near the break side but colour is irrelevant. Aid: first opposite-coloured bar in the pullback is the likely signal bar.
- Trigger: market order at 1 pip beyond the signal bar's extreme (e.g. short: signal low minus 1 pip).
- Signal bar must be small enough: "no taller than 7 pip" (7 pip bar + 2 pip for the break on each side... + 1 pip spread = 10 pip stop). This implies the STOP sits beyond the signal bar's far extreme with +1 pip margin, and the trade is taken only if that distance <= 10 pip. For very tiny signal bars (1 pip) the stop is widened slightly, using technical support, never indiscriminately.
- Slippage: if filled 1-1.5 pip worse, keep the trade and managing as normal (target 10 from the actual fill).
- Missed entry: re-entry allowed within the first couple of bars on a retest of the break level, never chase.
- In very fast news markets accept that a full 10 pip stop can be hit; do not trade if spreads widen.
- Second pullbacks: normally invalid for FB (only for exceptional quality, and he suggests skipping).
- Reasoning for skipping FB in favour of SB: a failed FB is read by countertrend traders as a signal to attack again; the FB trade then gets scratched. Lower odds than SB.
- Required win rate: with avg win 10 pip and avg loss about 6 pip, hit-rate of the target needed is about 40 percent (p. 76).
- Entry at "more than 50 percent chance" is enough; do not cherry-pick; take them all.

### Ch 9 - Second Break, SB (pp. 79-108)
- Pattern: trend (can be moderate) -> orderly, preferably diagonal pullback to the EMA -> first break (FB) of a signal bar in trend direction that FAILS to run (no follow-through) -> price comes back up/down toward the EMA (second push of the pullback; M shape in a downtrend, W shape in an uptrend) -> a second signal bar gets broken in the trend direction = SB entry.
- Number of bars between first and second break: 1-4 typical (compact), but may be elongated. Both breaks must be in the same pullback/EMA area.
- Entry: market order 1 pip beyond the second signal bar's extreme. Stop/target 10/10 bracket (stop beyond the pattern, scratch earlier).
- Quality conditions: the pullback low (for longs) of the first break should be tested and hold (second low of the W, "to the pip" or close); the SB entry should not be ~7 pip above the first break's entry without a test of the earlier low or EMA (that = low-probability, skip, p. 96); strong pullback with all same-colour bars into a strong opposite trend tends to exhaust itself (still OK for SB). Reinforcers: pullback tests a broken earlier level, round number zone, false break of a previous high just before the SB (stop hunters trapped).
- Do not wait for a round number to crack before entering SB (worse stop distance); "better to be short a pip above a round number than a pip below".
- Do NOT buy the first test of a broken support from below (resistance now) - lowest probability trade; the mirror is a short entry.
- A new setup appearing while in a position: ignore it (author's preference). Rationale: maximum risk per trade; two positions in one direction are one position entered twice and violate the per-trade risk. (Rule for us: single position per symbol.)
- Missed entry: usually a second chance within the first couple of bars (retest of broken level); but not if price developed a reversal pattern in between.
- Re-entry after a stop is expected: after a loss immediately take the next valid setup in the same direction (burnt-finger syndrome is the enemy).
- Valid trade as long as price keeps moving toward target, or at least does not take out specific highs/lows of the setup (rule of thumb for exit, full detail in Ch 14).

### Ch 10 - Block Break, BB (pp. 109-135)
- Pattern: a tight cluster ("block", "miniature range") of bars with a clear top and bottom barrier; ideally several touches ("equal highs") of the barrier on the side of the intended break; coil under pressure. Often 5-7 equal touches. Box height in the examples is as small as 3-5 pip.
- Where it appears: (1) end of a pullback near the EMA (can look countertrend if the pullback was long); (2) as a horizontal pullback in a very strong trend that does not retrace diagonally; (3) in non-trending markets, at tops/bottoms and in consolidations, with-trend or counter-trend.
- Signal line = the barrier on the side of the intended break (barrier can be 1-2 pip below the absolute high if several bars share that level; spiky single highs may be ignored). Entry = market order the moment price trades beyond the signal line (examples enter ~1 pip beyond).
- Direction filter: trade only the side in line with overall pressure: more distinctive higher bottoms than lower tops = long only, and vice versa. A higher bottom inside the box is a strong tell. Break against the pressure: no action.
- Pre-breakout tension (key concept): 2-6 small dojis/bars hugging the signal line right before the break (a "mini box in the box"); the rising/flat EMA seems to push bars out of the box. "The most reliable breakouts are those where the average literally pushes the bars out of the box."
- Reaction after break can be hesitant; price often crawls back in the box; stay in as long as the EMA caps / pushes (do not scratch inside the box for 1-3 pip). Breaks are tested back to the exact pip often.
- Overhead resistance to the left is less of a problem for BB than for DD because the BB itself arises because of that resistance; a break clears it.
- Countertrend BB (Fig 10.7): needs the break right after a significant support gets taken out, signal line = 5 equal lows, first real support (obstacle) at least about 10 pip away, and buildup before the break (a break with no buildup after a move from the top of the pattern straight to the bottom = false break trap). Even then, lacking miniature tension, it is borderline.
- A break that follows a drop from the other side of the range straight into the barrier ("no buildup") is typically a false break -> skip.
- Author's comment: since BB frequency is high (4 in an hour in Fig 10.5), 30 pip in an hour is achievable in a trend.
- After a trade is open, second box in same direction: ignore; if the first trade hits target exactly as the second box cracks, re-enter on a retest.
- Position sizing note (p. 121-122): all trades get the same maximum allowed volume (2 percent risk when consistently profitable, 1 percent while learning); no scaling by perceived quality.

### Ch 11 - Range Break, RB (pp. 137-171 and beyond)
- Range: sideways phase between a horizontal top and bottom, ideally >= 2 equal tops and >= 2 equal bottoms. Duration on 70-tick: about 15 minutes to a couple of hours, best part of an hour typical; slow Asian ranges can take 2.5 hours.
- Barrier drawing: usually the absolute extremes, but may squeeze the box 1-2 pip inward where most equal touches are (volatile markets), while in slow markets use the absolute extremes. Barrier on the break side is the "streamlined" one; the other side may be choppy. Barriers are adjusted as range develops; a barrier earns significance only after a second touch.
- Round number zones (00, 50, and also the 20-levels 20/40/60/80 as light guidance) often make up the range; EURUSD tends to move in 20-pip steps (break 1.3120 -> retest -> next stop 1.3140). Range barrier does not have to coincide with the round number (barrier 4-5 pip above 1.3650 is still tradable, p. 167).
- Two traps:
  - False break trap: price travels straight from one barrier to the other and breaks it at once without buildup (no pre-breakout tension). Skip; it gets slammed back.
  - Tease break trap: some tension but no proper "squeeze" - the move originates from the middle or from the EMA, not from a series of bounces between the EMA and the barrier. Skip when possible (but market is more forgiving; EMA often guides it out again). If a tease is pushed back and then later breaks out again, enter on the break of the ORIGINAL barrier (unless several teases together form a new barrier, e.g. false-break low F plus tease low T = new lower barrier, Fig 11.6).
- Valid RB: the "squeeze" = bars sandwiched between the 20 EMA and the barrier for at least a handful of bars (ideally price bounces between barrier and EMA a few times before being squeezed out); EMA sloping toward the break; underlying pressure (higher bottoms for an up-break). Entry = market order at the barrier break (1 pip beyond). Stop: squeezes give clean stop placement (hence valid RB are better than hasty ones).
- Countertrend range breaks need more clues: "the odd vs the obvious" - if the obvious with-trend break fails (false with-trend break), a subsequent counter-trend break gets more credible (Fig 11.6/11.9). Rule: only trade breaks that "leave absolutely no room for dispute".
- Round number "vacuum": breaking a range with a round number 10-13 pip beyond entry makes the 10 pip target more probable; but do not stretch the target (see math above).
- Breaks of a round number often fail without follow-through, then the opposite range break wins; failed breaks unwind trapped positions in a string of same-colour bars (which are the winners).
- News: bars print 10x faster before/after a release, spikes of 50+ pip, support/resistance evaporates, spreads widen up to 10 pip; avoid scalping around major releases (NFP, rate decisions) or accept larger stop slippage. Check the economic calendar.
- Do not look at higher time frame (daily patterns create bias); do not read market opinions.

## Topic tags

- trend: with-trend pullback setups only in clearly visible trend (EMA sloping, majority of bars in direction, bars a bit taller than average); trend hierarchy is relative; first pullback of a new trend is special (FB); "trend equals trend" heuristic; higher-bottoms / lower-tops count as a pressure read.
- breakout: BB and RB are breakout-from-compression systems; quality depends on pre-breakout tension, squeeze between EMA and barrier, tested barrier (multiple equal touches), no chart resistance; false-break and tease traps; breakouts are usually retested within a few bars.
- mean-reversion: only in a sense of pullback-to-EMA entries in a trend (40-60 percent retracement); countertrend scalps allowed only with strong clues, small expected move.
- momentum/oscillators: none; only the 20 EMA slope/position. Strong bars (long bodies closing on extremes) mark the surge.
- patterns: doji clusters, M/W patterns, flags, double tops, head and shoulders appear but are only "named after the fact"; the rules are about bar relations (signal bar break by 1 pip).
- intraday/sessions: Asian session slow (fewer ticks, long ranges 2.5 h), Europe/US active; news bursts; avoid releases; ranges more common near round numbers; late Friday/holiday = dead market. No explicit time-of-day trading windows.
- seasonality/time: none beyond session activity.
- volatility: bar height 2-4 pip calm, 5+ trending; signal bar must be <= about 7 pip so a 10 pip stop fits; compression of the DD bars vs preceding bars is a quality filter; tall setups make scratches expensive; news = huge range, avoid.
- stops-and-exits: fixed 10 pip target (limit), 10 pip max stop, stop only moved toward target to scratch a trade (tipping point Ch 14); stop normally behind the setup's far extreme; average loss about 6 pip because of scratches; trade stays valid while price does not take out setup highs/lows. Do not widen the target.
- position-sizing/risk: 1 percent per trade while learning, up to 2 percent when consistently profitable; always use the max volume allowed by that risk; one position at a time; volume determined from stop distance (10 pip stop = twice the volume of a 20 pip stop). Detail in Ch 16 (second half).
- testing-and-robustness/overfitting: he claims no stats. Says human execution makes backtested edges vanish; "marginal edge under ideal circumstances" should be avoided; needs hundreds of trades to judge variants; do not change rules after single outcomes.
- costs/execution: 1 pip spread = 10 percent of the 10 pip target; market orders with slippage 1-1.5 pip possible; separate chart feed that matches the bid/ask; widen of spread in news = no trade.
- psychology/discipline: heavy; no prediction, no bias, take every valid trade, accept loss, re-enter immediately after a stop, do not chase, do not watch P/L, boredom in ranges.
- portfolio/diversification: none (single instrument).

## Testable ideas (our data: M1 bars only, no ticks)

### Conversion of the tick chart to M1
- 70 ticks "resembles a 30-second chart" in active hours (his text, p. 9). In his charts the time axis labels run about every 3-5 minutes per ~6-8 bars -> roughly 20-40 s per bar in London/NY, much slower in Asia, 13x faster in news.
- Closest we can get: M1 bars (2 x slower than 30 s) or a synthetic "constant-activity" bar. We have no tick counts, so options: (a) plain M1 bars; (b) M1 bars only in the active window (London 07:00-16:00 UTC, NY 12:00-21:00 UTC) and skipped in Asia; (c) pseudo tick bars built from M1 using volume proxy = number of M1 bars with a minimum range (e.g. bar every X pips of cumulative M1 high-low path). Recommendation: start with M1 in London/NY and with EMA length 20 on M1 (covers 20 minutes instead of his ~10 minutes), then test EMA 10/14/20/34 to see sensitivity. Pattern widths scale: a DD of two 30 s bars becomes about one M1 bar - so DD as "two dojis" does not map well to M1; the equivalent is an "inside bar / narrow range bar" at the EMA. M5 might be too slow (bar count down 10x); M1 and M2/M3 are the realistic frames.
- Histdata M1 is presumably bid-based; spread must be added in the cost model. "Equal highs" tolerance: the book rounds to whole pips; we should round highs/lows to 1 pip (EURUSD) or a configurable tolerance in ATR units to detect "equal extremes".

### Do 10 pip brackets survive our costs?
(All FX/gold characteristic numbers below are my rough assumptions and must be measured on our data and with our spread model; EURUSD figures are from the book.)
- EURUSD (book): spread 1 pip = 10 percent of the 10 pip target; ADR about 150 pip so target = about 0.07 of daily ATR; M1 ATR in active hours is ~1.5-3 pip so 10 pip is about 4-6 M1 ATR. Win loss payoff: +10 pip win, about -6 pip average loss (after scratches), break-even hit rate about 40 percent including spread (his own statement).
- GBPUSD: ADR about 100-130 pip, retail spread about 1-1.5 pip -> a 10 pip bracket would cost 10-15 percent of target; M1 ATR about 2-3.5 pip. Roughly the same structure as EURUSD, slightly worse cost ratio but more movement per bar; in ATR terms use target = 4-6 x M1-ATR(14) and stop the same, and require spread <= 12 percent of target.
- GBPJPY: ADR about 150-200 pip, spread about 2-3 pip -> 10 pip bracket costs 20-30 percent of target: not viable at 10/10; scale to the same cost ratio: target about 20-30 pip (= 4-6 M1 ATR) so spread <= 10 percent.
- XAUUSD: in "pips" (0.10 USD) ADR about 200-300, spread about 20-35 cents = 2-3.5 pips -> 10 pip target = USD 1.0, cost 20-35 percent; need target about USD 2.5-4 (= 4-6 M1 ATR) to reach the 10 percent cost ratio.
- General rule to implement: define stop = k_stop x ATR_M1(14) and target = k_tp x ATR_M1(14) with k about 4-6 (derived from EURUSD), then compute spread/target and skip any trade where spread/target > 0.10-0.15 (cost-aware filter). Test with spread modeled per symbol and with slippage 0.5-1 pip. The book's break-even hit rate of 40 percent applies only if payoff ratio about 1.67 (10 win / 6 loss); in a fixed 1:1 bracket without scratches need > 50 percent plus cost: 1 pip cost on 10/10 -> break-even about 55 percent. So the scratch (tipping point) rule is part of the claimed edge and must be implemented in test (Ch 14, second half).

### Idea list
1. **DD pullback-to-EMA micro break (M1 version)** - Trend = EMA(20) slope up (e.g. EMA[t] - EMA[t-5] > x ATR) and price above EMA for most of the last N bars; pullback = price moved back 40-60 percent of the last swing and touches/pierces EMA; signal bar = narrow bar(s) (range <= 0.4 x average bar range of the previous 10 bars) at the EMA, two or more within a few pips of each other (trend-side extremes within 1 pip or 0.1 x ATR); trigger = stop order 1 pip (or 0.1 ATR) beyond signal bar's high (long) / low (short); stop = beyond the doji group's far extreme + 1 pip but max 10 pip (cap by k x ATR); target = 10 pip equivalent (about 5 ATR); skip if a cluster of bars or a 00/50 round number lies within the target distance on the left (check price level density over last 90 min). Source Ch 7 p. 39-60. Evidence: none quantitative; screenshots only. Fit: moderate for GBPUSD, poor for GBPJPY/XAU at fixed pips (use ATR scaling); M1 loses the "two dojis" resolution. Differs from failed strategies: no session zone, no SMC concepts, micro-timeframe pullback entry, fixed small target (previous strategies hold longer).
2. ★ **Block break with pre-breakout tension (BB/RB generic compression breakout)** - Detect a box: last N (e.g. 10-40 M1 bars) with range <= m x ATR, >= 3 touches (within 1 pip) of the top barrier (or bottom); direction filter = higher lows in the box for a long (or EMA slope up / price above EMA), bars squeezed between EMA(20) and barrier (>= 5 bars closing in the top 30 percent of the box, last 2-3 bars small); entry = stop order 1 pip above barrier; stop = box low (or just under the last higher low) capped at 10 pip equivalent; target = 1R to 1.7R; skip if the move to the barrier came directly from the other side of the box in a single leg (false break trap) or round number is within 1R beyond. Source Ch 10-11 p. 109-171. Evidence: many chart examples, claims of "hardly a session without it". Fit: good - range compression/breakout can be fully coded on M1, scale by ATR, works on all three FX/gold symbols; ranges also on indices. Differs from failures: not SMC sweep; not session-zone based; price-pattern compression plus EMA squeeze with strict entry filters. This is also close to what we may already have in generic breakout but the "squeeze + tension + no false break" filters are new.
3. ★ **Second break (SB, M/W pattern at EMA)** - In a trend (EMA slope), pullback to the EMA; first break signal bar fails to run (price returns to within 1-3 bars toward the EMA without making a new extreme and without taking out the pullback extreme by more than 1 pip); second signal bar broken = entry 1 pip beyond the second signal bar's extreme; stop beyond the pattern's low/high (pullback extreme) +1 pip, max 10 pip; target 10 pip equiv; reject if the SB entry is more than 7 pip beyond the first break entry (no test of the earlier extreme). Source Ch 9 p. 79-108. Evidence: author's preferred setup (favoured over FB); no stats. Fit: good on M1 (swing-based pattern, not tick dependent). Differs: classic two-leg pullback entry vs. our failed trend following (stop-and-reverse, no TP) and SMC entries.
4. ★ **Cost-aware ATR-scaled scalping bracket** - A meta rule: for any of the above entries use target = 5 x ATR_M1(14), stop = structure-based and capped at 5 x ATR, skip when spread > 12 percent of target; with scratch rule from Ch 14 (second half) ("exit if price takes out setup extreme") test hit rate vs. break-even 40 percent. This tests whether any of Volman's setups survive our spreads on GBPUSD/GBPJPY/XAUUSD. Fit: key question for the whole method; likely fails on GBPJPY/XAU at small targets, maybe survives GBPUSD in London/NY overlap. Differs: very short holding period, no swap cost, trade only active hours.
5. **First break after a violent surge (FB)** - Surge = >= 3 bars with range > 1.5 x ATR closing within 20 percent of extremes in one direction, often exiting a range of >= 30 bars; first pullback of 40-70 percent with no bar broken in the surge direction until the signal bar; entry 1 pip beyond the signal bar; signal bar <= 0.7 x stop; stop beyond signal bar + 1; target 10 pip equiv. Source Ch 8. Evidence: none; author himself says FB is rarely tradable and often inferior. Fit: fewer trades, news-driven (hard to filter, higher spread) -> poor-to-moderate. Differs: momentum burst + first pullback rule.
6. **Range break with squeeze (RB), barrier at equal tops/bottoms near 00/50 round numbers** - Detect range of >= 30 min (>= 2 equal tops and >= 2 equal bottoms within 1 pip), price squeezed between EMA(20) and barrier for >= 5 bars with at least 2 bounces EMA<->barrier before the break (to exclude tease), no straight run from the opposite barrier; entry stop order 1 pip beyond barrier; stop at the last squeeze low/high (pivot) capped; target 1-1.5 x range height or fixed 10 pip equiv. Source Ch 11. Fit: good, symmetrical long/short, all symbols; uses round-number distance which is natural for XAU (50/100 USD levels) and indices. Differs from failure 1: price-only range break, no FVG/OB/BOS; from failure 2: not NY open specific, from 3: not candle shape.
7. **Round-number vacuum effect filter** - Add-on: after a break, if the next 00/50 (or 20-level) round number is within 1.0-1.5 x target distance in trade direction and there is no cluster in between, increase probability (author's claim); conversely if a cluster/round number sits within 0.5 x target, skip. Test as a feature/filter on ideas 1-3 and 6 (does conditional hit rate differ?). Source Ch 7, 10, 11 p. 57-59, 154-155. Fit: very testable on XAU, FX; indices levels at 100/50 points. Differs: it is a filter, not a strategy.
8. **Skip-the-news filter and spread filter** - Avoid trading in the 15 min around high impact releases and when bar speed (number of M1 ticks if available / range) is > 3 x normal; check effect on all ideas. Source Ch 8, 11. Fit: needs an economic calendar we may not have; proxy with M1 range spikes (> 4 x ATR bars) or fixed times (13:30 UTC US data, FOMC). Differs: risk filter.
9. **Re-entry after stop in same setup direction** - After a stopped-out setup, accept the next valid setup in the same direction immediately; compare expectancy of "re-entry" vs. "one attempt per swing". Source Ch 7, 9 (p. 47, 93-95). Fit: trivial to test. Differs: sequential logic.
10. **Missed-entry retest** - If the break bar closes beyond the trigger without fill (or price gap), allow entry on retest of the broken level within the next 2-3 bars if no reversal pattern formed. Source Ch 8-10. Fit: needs intrabar logic; M1 limit-ish approximated. Differs: execution rule.

## Warnings / pitfalls
- No statistics in the book: no hit rates, no expectancy, no sample; the only numbers are 10/10 bracket, avg loss about 6 pip and need for about 40 percent target hits. Everything else is discretionary judgement on screenshots (survivorship: chosen "textbook" examples).
- Author states that backtests of such methods give better results than live because of human effects; he warns against marginal edges. In our backtest, the discretionary filters (chart resistance, "quality" of pullback, squeeze/tease) are the real edge and are hard to code without leaking hindsight. Be careful about lookahead when defining equal highs, "squeeze" and "first pullback of a new trend" (surge origin).
- Costs: a 1 pip spread already eats 10 percent of the target; on GBPJPY and XAUUSD the same pip-based brackets are not viable; costs rise with news and in Asia. Slippage of 1-1.5 pip occurs in fast markets, and for stop orders ("market order when hit") the loss is worse than 10 pip.
- Tick bars versus time bars: M1 bars hide the detail on which the signal-bar/entry-bar logic relies (entry bar gap of 1 pip, signal bars 1 pip tall); a stop order 1 pip beyond the signal bar can be triggered and reversed inside one M1 bar - need worst-case assumption (SL hit first when both inside one bar).
- Equal-extreme detection depends on rounding to whole pips; pipette-level data produces "jagged" highs and misses signals.
- The approach is intraday and needs many trades per day; commission/spread friction multiplies; results from pattern-trading with 10 pip targets are very sensitive to spread assumptions - test with spread x1, x1.5, x2.
- Author's own caveats: do not trade into round numbers with DD; do not trade FB in second pullbacks; skip tease and false-break entries; avoid news; do not chase; do not double up (one position).
- Second half (not read by this agent) contains the actual exit logic (tipping point), IRB/ARB, unfavourable conditions and money management; these are required to judge the system as a whole, since the scratch rule is what makes average loss about 6 pip rather than 10.
