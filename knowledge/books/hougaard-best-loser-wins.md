# Tom Hougaard - "Best Loser Wins" (Harriman House, 2022) - notes

Note: the extracted text has no printed page numbers; locations are given as chapter + section heading (and approx. txt line number "L").

## Overview
- Psychology book by a high-stake UK/Danish discretionary day trader of DAX, Dow (Wall Street 30), FTSE (also Brent, GBPUSD) on 5m/10m charts, ex-CFD broker strategist (City Index). Not a strategy book; he states his setups work "about 70%" at best and that edge is not the point.
- Core thesis: 80-90% of retail CFD clients lose because they behave the same way (hope on losers, fear on winners). Do the opposite: assume you are wrong, always use a stop, cut losers fast, never add to losers, add to winners, never use profit targets, trade with the trend (buy strength, sell weakness).
- Concrete, testable content is thin and scattered: (1) pressing/adding to winners with ATR-spaced add-ons, (2) no profit target, stop-based trailing, (3) a few Dow/DAX day-of-week / first-hour / gap / trend-day observations (stated as facts, no proof given), (4) one inside-bar double-top short example, (5) a "harmonic retracement" Brent entry, (6) statistics on his own trading and on retail clients.
- He gives no systematic rule set. Most rules are anecdotal or discretionary; I mark "VAGUE" wherever the rule cannot be coded as stated.
- Evidence level: author's own live Telegram track record (not independently verified), anecdotes, one retail-broker study (43M FX trades). No backtests presented.

## Chapter notes

### Introduction / Liar's Poker / Trading Floor (L214-1190)
- Retail failure rates posted by EU brokers (Nov 2019): IG 75%, Markets.com 89%, CMC 75%, Saxo 74%, FXPro 77%.
- Broker CEO's five markers of a good vs bad client: account size, trade frequency (overtrading loses), holding time winners vs losers, adding to winners (good) vs adding to losers (near-certain ruin), use of a stop-loss. Clients win more often than they lose but lose about 1.66 per 1 won.
- Spreads: DAX ~0.9 pts today vs 6-8 in 1999; Dow 1 pt vs 8. (Cost insight: cost per trade is much lower now, yet failure rate unchanged.)
- Stake: he risks 100 to 3,500 GBP per point; once made 17k in <7s, lost 29k in 8s.

### Ch "Everyone Is a Chart Expert" (L1816-3695)
- Charts are naked: no indicators at all. Indicators are "distortions" of price/time; work only in one regime (trend vs range). Uses 10-minute chart for most day trading; 5m also.
- Retail error patterns he saw on the floor: buying lows in downtrends, shorting highs in uptrends, treating every small counter-move as a new trend (e.g. 15 Sep 2008 Lehman day, Dow -4.5%: clients bought every green 5m candle).
- 43M-trade FX study (David Rodriguez, 25,000 clients, 15 months): 62% winners, avg win ~43 pips, avg loss ~78-83 pips (he quotes 78 once, 83 later; "66% more on losses" once). Lose more per loss than win per win -> net loser. Hit rate alone is meaningless.
- Hedge fund friend Trevor Neil used DeMark Sequential on 1-minute chart on South African shares: hit rate 25-30% (up to 40% when hot, mid-20s cold), winners ~25x risk, trade must "work immediately" else closed (small loss); backtest-derived rule. Closed after a few years, returned money.
- Gap statistic claim: only 48% of gaps fill same day; 76% by third day (source not given; DAX). Do not assume gaps always fill.
- Fibonacci demonstration: Larry Williams' study of S&P hourly retracements over ~10 years showed no clustering at 38.2/61.8; Hougaard himself found DAX 78.6% retracement has about 20% hit rate but can be profitable with tiny risk and large payoff (Oslo talk 2016; no numbers beyond that).
- Candlestick patterns: cites two academic papers finding hammer/shooting star and most reversal patterns have mean returns not distinguishable from zero.
- Dow daily stat: over ~30 years (~7,500 days) only 50.4% of closes were above the previous close despite 3,300 -> 36,000 rise. So daily direction is a coin flip; short-term TF cannot rely on higher-TF trend.
- Value-vs-price: markets have no "cheap/expensive"; overbought/oversold indicators are the same illusion. "Buy high sell higher; sell low cover lower." Trends persist: odds above 50% that current movement continues (assertion, no numbers).

### Ch "Fighting My Humanness" (L3696-5567)
- Table of seven loss-making behaviors (hoping, indicator excuses, "can't go broke taking profit", reducing size when winning, increasing when losing, quitting after making daily points, trading without conviction). Size should go UP when winning, not down.
- IG client sentiment 26 Oct 2021: Dow 71% net short on a day of all-time highs, DAX 63% short, FTSE 69% short, US500 60% short. (Contrarian-sentiment idea: retail is wrong-footed in trends.)
- "Know what the market can do" volatility prep (Figs 9-11, DAX/FTSE intraday): he decomposes the day into swings: first leg up 24 pts, retracements 9, 12, 17 pts, small pullbacks in strong trend 3-7 pts, deeper ones 7-12 (one 14-17 outlier). Rule: buy the pullback after an impulse (+11), expect retracement up to ~12 pts max, put stop just beyond (~12+). Idea = use recent typical retracement size as stop distance; VAGUE (numbers specific to those days, scaled by ATR in his later formula).
- Charlie DiFrancesca (CBOT bond pit): you become a good trader when you first hold and ADD to a winner. Dr David Paul's advice: when in profit think "where do I add" not "where do I exit".
- Why adding to losers is easy and winners hard (average price logic). He adds same size each time (own practice) but recommends decreasing size on later adds for beginners.
- Adding example (Dow): long 26,629, stop 26,590 (39 pts); at 26,649 adds again; moves stop on first position up to 26,629 so both positions share one stop (first at breakeven, second loses 20 pts if hit). Dow had rallied from base 26,569 before entry - he's fine joining a trend already running ("late to the party") as long as a stop exists.
- Four things the 90% do that he reverses: they do not add to winners; they avoid stops; they add to losers; they take half profit (half at 1R, move stop to BE, rest at 2R). He rejects scale-outs: "never gives the home-run trades", no target. He defines risk only, never reward ("no risk:reward").
- Entry example (DAX, 1 Oct 2019 short, "Everyone is a chart expert"): DAX gapped up; bar 1 = first 10-min bar (high equals a prior high -> double top); bar 2 is an inside bar; bar 3 closes below the low of the inside bar -> sell, stop above (about 20 pts risk suggested by his 20-pt example). Stop to breakeven after profit, no target; liquidity vacuum later gave a big move. Rule is one example, not formalised.
- "How not to do it" (DAX gap-up): short the "low bar" after gap-up, stop near day high, risk 35 pts, stopped out; then reverse long on the close of the bar above his stop ("stop and reverse"), add on consolidation breakout, but closed early to offset prior loss -> missed afternoon rally. Takeaways: on a gap-up day odds favor continuation over reversal (claim); "in bull trends resistance is often broken, in bear trends support rarely holds"; claims 90% of daily highs/lows occur in the first 1.5 hours (VAGUE, unsourced).
- Position sizing formula for pressing winners (FTSE example): N = ATR on the chosen intraday TF (e.g. 10 pts for FTSE 5m during session; night ATR ~4, 8:00 GMT open ~14, so use session-specific ATR). Stop = 2N. Risk = 2% of account (10,000 -> 200). Unit size = risk / (2N) = 10 GBP per point. Add one unit every +0.5N (he says "every N" in text but the example adds at 5 points = N/2 = half the ATR, i.e. note inconsistency in the book: N=10, add spacing 5). When adding, move the stop of earlier units up by the add spacing so all units share the same stop; total risk in example 35 pts (20 + 15) then falls as stops advance. Warns this can produce larger-than-planned loss; recommends smaller later adds. Directly backtestable (Turtle-like pyramiding on intraday).
- Trend-day definition: opens at the day's high or low and closes at the opposite extreme (opens at high, closes at low or vice versa). Occurs "on at least 20% of trading days in stock indices" (claim). Cannot be known in advance. His Dow research (18 years): first-hour precursors; one precursor = gap down after a gap up day, gap not filled in first hour. Fridays, especially start or end of month, "notorious" for trend days. Final-hour rule: never short a market making a new high of the day in the last hour; on a Friday/month-end trend day buy new highs late and add (Dow Oil-day anecdote).
- Dow example short (Friday after a positive Thursday): scaled in 5 entries at point 1, +25% at point 2, +10% at point 3; total 3,000 DKK/pt (~500 USD/pt) at avg 25,419.6, price 25,135.9 (~284 pts profit); stops moved down to breakeven; target none. Profit-taking via chart: double bottom against position, OR "place stop where I would want to enter the other way" (stop-and-reverse level = the point at which he would be a buyer). Retained part of paper profit; he accepts giving back a lot.
- Failure example: DAX short at 11,288 scaled/added on the way down then added again at old top as market reversed; short 4,500 DKK/pt, closed -25 pts: shows pyramiding losses when reversal is sharp (the downside of adding).
- Paul Tudor Jones anecdote: sells a market that has fallen 100 points in 15 minutes ("if it keeps going down I'd sell it to zero"). Momentum/breakdown continuation attitude.
- "Option 1-4" framework: only ask what hurts more; missing a planned entry hurts more than a stop-out for him.

### Ch "Disgust" and "The Drifter Mind" (L5568-6236)
- Motivation chapters. Self-review: plotted every trade on a chart after the day; saw 80% impulse trades; list of his early habits: overtrading from boredom or anger, jumping the gun, fighting the trend (catching lows), cutting winners, averaging down.
- Warm-up: PowerPoint of his own trades (good and bad) every morning; visualization of losses; breath 7 s in / 11 s out.
- Friday/month-end Dow day (L6137-6236): Dow +200 open, oil crashing, he shorted, reversed long, reversed short, stopped, then bought reluctantly as Dow made new highs, moved stops up, added more; "60 min to go and Dow makes a new high of the day -> don't short, expect close at the high tick". Says he lost 4 days in a row once that week; do not stop trading after 3 losses (only stop if ill/emotional).
- Quote (Greg Riba, pit trader): when winning, bet more.

### Ch "Trading Through a Slump" (L6238-6622)
- Friend Adam: 30-minute chart index system (George Taylor "Taylor Trading Technique" inspired): check chart every 30 minutes, trade only if parameters met, sometimes no trade all day; lost everything after holding a max-short over a weekend (Saddam capture, limit-up Sunday open) -> gap/overnight risk and margin close-out.
- His May slump: from +200k GBP month, lost >50% in a week (33k, 9k, 38k losses); biggest ever position 400 FTSE futures at 4,000 GBP/pt, short a "big fall off the open, 2-3 five-minute bars of rebound, then new lows" pattern which failed that day. (This is a named pattern: gap/open drop -> 2-3 bar pullback -> continuation lower.)
- Process orientation: stay with the same process through slumps.

### Ch "Embracing Failure" (L6624-6888)
- His stats for May 2020: 137 trades, 53 winners, 66 losers, 18 break-even (stop moved to entry), net +1,513 points. So hit rate 39% wins / 48% losers / 13% BE; profitable via asymmetry. Average trade about +11 points (1,513/137).
- "You can go broke taking a profit" if you cannot let profits run.
- Exit strategy: stop-loss defines loss, accepted before the day starts; no target.

### Ch "Best Loser Wins" (L6890-7300)
- His setups "work at best about 70%"; typical good systems have ~60% strike rate; he is wrong ~30-50% of the time.
- Four rules: (1) assume I'm wrong until proven right (expect to exit losers quickly, another trade will come), (2) expect to be uncomfortable, (3) add when right, (4) never add when wrong. Be small when wrong, big when right.
- Loss-aversion: losses felt 250% as intensely as equal gains (quoted neuroscience claim).

### Ch "The Ideal Mindset" (L7302-8683)
- Coin-flip logic: 50% win rate with 1 unit loss and 1.5 unit wins is a good business; judge over ~100 trades, not one. 15 heads in a row seen in his own 100 flips.
- "Book of Truths": his statistical self-audit of his trade log (in Excel, split by time of day, weekday, holding time, win/loss). Findings: win rate once above 85% in periods; average winner < average loser; big losses damaged P&L; good first half of the day, good Mon-Thu, gave back profit in afternoons and on Fridays; excelled on range days; missed or fought trend days; biggest losses from fighting trends. Result: trade fewer hours (mornings), less is more. Time-of-day and weekday effects are for him personally, not for the market.
- Trust the market: reviewed ten years of intraday data for the products he trades and concluded that in a ten-hour session (120 five-minute bars) at least 2-3 bars would produce a good entry; example entries: double top in a higher-TF downtrend, or continuation signal. Prints 5m and 10m charts nightly; 10m slows decisions and reduces overtrading.
- Brent example (4 Mar 2022): "Harmonic Retracement" setup on 10-minute chart: first and second retracement are of identical size, buy at end of the second equal pullback in the trend; "razor-sharp entries, easy to control risk" (VAGUE: no measurement rule beyond equal-size retracement). Bad example: impulsive entry on first pullback without a defined stop.
- Belief example rule: "wait for the first 10-minute bar to complete before deciding to trade" (open-bar rule).
- Dr David Paul's "Twenty Trades" exercise: take 20 consecutive signals without skipping; goal is emotional detachment, expect about breakeven.
- Dow trade example with 200-point profit left to run at an old low rather than take profit.
- Trade management summary: stop always placed at entry; no profit targets; stop-loss trails/moves to breakeven; add on strength; exit on chart reversal signals (double top/bottom against) or on being stopped; reverse direction quickly when wrong (stop-and-reverse mentality, e.g. long 12,808/12,818 index, three major lows on 5m = "get out bars" if against you, "add to bars" if with you; reversed to short and won it back).
- Claims: no losing day since Sept 2021 (seven months) at time of writing; 39 straight non-losing days earlier; "325,000 GBP in the last month" (unverified).

## Topic tags
- trend: buy strength/sell weakness; do not fade; gap-up day continuation more likely than fill (claim); small counter moves are not trend changes; higher-TF trend not very predictive for 5m (Dow closes up only 50.4% of days in 30y).
- breakout: new day high late in session = continuation (do not short, expect close at high); inside bar break (DAX, 10m) as entry; breakout of consolidation to add.
- mean-reversion: strongly rejected for retail ("cheap/expensive" illusion, overbought/oversold). Only exception noted: Fibonacci 78.6% retracement has ~20% hit rate with tiny risk and big payoff (his DAX study, no data shown).
- momentum/oscillators: no indicators used; oscillators called harmful in trends. Momentum-chasing attitude (PTJ story).
- patterns: double top/bottom, inside bar break, equal-size second retracement ("harmonic retracement"), open drop -> 2-3 bar bounce -> new lows, gap down after gap-up day not filled in first hour = trend-day precursor. All discretionary. Candlestick names considered meaningless (academic evidence cited).
- intraday/sessions: primary TF 5m and 10m; ATR varies a lot by session (FTSE 5m ATR 4 pts at night vs 14 at 8:00 GMT open); first 1.5 hours supposed to contain 90% of day extremes (claim); wait for first 10-minute bar; performs better mornings than afternoons (personal); trend days ~20% of days, favored Fridays and month start/end; US open influences DAX moves.
- seasonality/time: Friday and month-end/start trend-day tendency (claim, unquantified); his own Friday giveback; weekend gaps dangerous (Adam story).
- volatility: N = ATR on trading TF, session-specific; stop = 2N; add every N/2 (example) ; "know what the market can do" via recent swing/retracement sizes.
- stops-and-exits: always a stop at entry; move stop to breakeven when in profit; trail by chart levels or by add-on spacing; no profit target; place stop at the level where he would take the opposite trade (stop-and-reverse); reject partial profits; give back profit to capture rare big days (accept "100 pt winner -> zero"); loses 50% of trades yet profitable.
- position-sizing/risk: 2% of account per trade for the unit; same-size adds (his practice) vs decreasing adds (recommended for novices); never add to losers; increase size when winning, decrease when losing (the opposite of retail); position can be 3,000-4,500 DKK/pt on Dow/DAX built from 5-10 entries.
- testing-and-robustness/overfitting: selection bias with trend lines drawn after the fact, Fibonacci hype shown to be random clustering (S&P 2021 rally retracements were 43% and 74%, not Fib), candlestick academic studies, trust verified by ten-year intraday review; no formal out-of-sample testing described. Process: trade journal with chart plots; statistics by weekday and time of day.
- costs/execution: spread modest (DAX 0.9, Dow 1 point); frequent entry/exit means re-entry costs spread; uses stop orders; mentions some markets need orders because they move fast.
- psychology/discipline: whole book. Key ones: hope on losers, fear on winners; loss-aversion 2.5x; mental warm-up with losing-trade visualization; process orientation; 20-trades exercise; no revenge trading; do not stop after three losses; no goals/targets.
- portfolio/diversification: not discussed (he trades few index CFDs, Brent, GBPUSD).

## Testable ideas

1. ★ **ATR pyramiding of winners (Hougaard add-on scheme)** - Entry by any base signal (see 3/4). Stop = 2N from entry where N = ATR(14?) of the trading TF (he does not give the ATR length; use session-filtered ATR). Unit risk = 2% of equity / 2N. Add one equal unit every +0.5N in profit (book text says "every N" but worked example uses 5 pts with N=10; test both 0.5N and 1N); after each add, move all stops to the same level (previous stop + add spacing) so total risk never grows beyond the first unit and quickly turns to zero; exit when the common stop is hit; max adds not specified (VAGUE). - Source: Fighting My Humanness, "Pressing Winners" Steps 1-4 (L4803-4906). - Evidence: only anecdotes (Dow short +284 pts with 10 entries); no stats. - Fit: good on indices (NAS100, US500) 5m-15m intraday and flat by close (no swap); compare R profile of base signal with vs without pyramiding in R terms. Differs from failed strategies 1-4: strategy 4 had stop-and-reverse with no pyramiding; here the focus is convexity of position size, not the entry edge. It will not rescue a signal without edge but it amplifies positive tails (needs trend regime).

2. **No profit target, breakeven-then-trail exit** - After entry, hard stop at defined risk; once +1R (or +N) move stop to entry; thereafter trail with chart swing lows/highs (VAGUE, pick pivot-based trail from existing swing detector) or exit when opposite double-top/bottom forms. Never fixed TP. - Source: Fighting My Humanness, "Risk to Reward", DAX 1 Oct 2019 trade. - Evidence: May 2020: 137 trades, 39% winners, 13% break-even, 48% losers, +1,513 points. - Fit: good; testable on any base signal by comparing fixed 2R TP vs trailing exit. Differs: strategy 1 and 2 used fixed targets/TP obstacles.

3. ★ **Inside-bar break + double top/bottom at the open (DAX/US indices, 10m)** - Gap day. Bar1 = first 10-min bar of the cash session. Bar2 is an inside bar (high < bar1 high, low > bar1 low). Bar3 closes below bar2's low -> short (mirror for long); filter: bar1's extreme equals a prior high (double top; tolerance unspecified, VAGUE). Stop above the day's high (his example 20 pts on DAX); move stop to BE after profit; no target. - Source: "Everyone Is a Chart Expert", DAX trade (L1937-2000). - Evidence: single example. - Fit: good for NAS100/US500 using 10m bars after 09:30 NY or DAX-like open; no swap since intraday. Differs from strategy 2 (sweep of NY open) by using inside-bar break with explicit stop at day extreme; but also related to opening-range logic so risk of the same non-edge.

4. ★ **Opening-gap direction rule: gap-up continuation vs fill** - Claim: only 48% of gaps fill the same day; 76% by day 3; on gap-up days continuation odds beat reversal. Test: for NAS100/US500 (cash session gaps from M1 data), measure P(gap filled same day), by gap size in ATR units and by whether first-hour breaks the open-bar extreme; trade breakout of the first 10m bar (or first 30m range) in gap direction with stop at opposite extreme/2N. Also the precursor "gap down after a gap-up day not filled in the first hour = trend-day precursor". - Source: Everyone Is a Chart Expert; "How Not To Do It"; "Pressing Winners" trend day paragraph. - Evidence: stats quoted without source (48%, 76%, 90% of extremes in first 1.5 h, 20% trend days). - Fit: good for indices (and partly FX/gold daily open); all are verifiable in our data and cheap to test. Differs from failed ones: pure statistical gap/first-hour study, not SMC sweeps.

5. ★ **Trend-day detector with late-day continuation** - Define trend day by close position: open within bottom/top 10% of the day range and close in the opposite extreme (parameter unspecified; VAGUE). Test whether features at 60-90 min (gap not filled, new day high at hour N, Friday, month start/end) raise P(trend day) above his 20% claim, then trade only those with pyramiding (idea 1). Also last-hour rule: if a new high of the day prints in the last 60 minutes, expect close at/near the high (don't short; long with trail). - Source: Pressing Winners; Drifter Mind (Friday oil day). - Evidence: claims only. - Fit: good, uses only price; calendar split Friday/month-end is cheap. Differs: conditional on day-type, tests seasonality of structure, not an entry pattern.

6. **Open-drop / 2-3 bar bounce / new low continuation (FTSE, DAX)** - After a big drop off the open, count 2-3 five-minute bars of rebound, then short the break to new lows with stop above rebound high. - Source: Trading Through a Slump (L6526). - Evidence: mentioned as a pattern "seen many times"; it failed on his worst day. - Fit: plausible on NAS100/US500 intraday; very discretionary ("big fall" undefined).

7. **Equal-retracement ("harmonic retracement") continuation** - In an impulsive trend (10m), measure the first pullback size; enter long/short when the second pullback equals the first, stop beyond the pullback extreme. - Source: Ideal Mindset, Brent example (L7931). - Evidence: one chart. - Fit: moderate; testable with swing detector, equal tolerance must be defined (e.g. +-10-20%). Related to strategy 1/2 retracement logic but without zones; likely noise.

8. **Stop-and-reverse at the opposite entry level** - Put the stop where you would enter the opposite way; on stop-out reverse immediately in the same bar close; applies to intraday breakouts. - Source: Pressing Winners / How Not To Do It (L4743-4747, L5010-5014). - Evidence: anecdotes (DAX long after being stopped on short). - Fit: ok but strategy 4 already tested stop-and-reverse and failed with swap; test intraday only (no swap) with costs because reversals double spread cost.

9. **Retail-sentiment contrarian (net-short extremes in trending indices)** - Retail positioning (IG) 60-71% short on Dow/DAX/FTSE on all-time-high day; implies buying trends. Not testable with our data (no sentiment feed). Mention only as context supporting trend/momentum bias.

10. **Retracement-size stop calibration** - Use median of recent N pullbacks (e.g. last 3 retracements: 8, 7, 10 pts) times 1.2-1.5 as stop distance after buying a pullback in an impulsive leg; equivalent to ATR-based stop, so test 2N vs retracement-percentile stop. - Source: Fighting My Humanness, Figs 9-11. - Fit: good, small variant.

11. **Time-of-day and weekday filter (self-audit method)** - Not a market claim: run the same journal split (by hour, weekday, month-end, Friday) on any candidate strategy's R trades; expect morning > afternoon and Mon-Thu > Fri for intraday index systems to show up only if the market has such structure. Cheap robustness check; flagged because it matches the book's "Book of Truths".

12. **Fibonacci 78.6% retracement lottery** - Entry at 78.6% retracement of a prior swing in trend direction, very tight stop, large payoff; claimed ~20% hit rate and positive expectancy on DAX (Oslo 2016 talk, no data). - Source: Curse of Patterns, "Larry Pesavento vs Larry Williams". - Fit: easily testable with swing detector on all 5 symbols; skeptical as Hougaard himself shows Fib levels are not special for S&P retracements. Mean-reversion-with-trend structure, low hit rate.

## Warnings / pitfalls
- Hit rate vs payoff: retail traders won 61-62% of trades but lost: avg win ~43 pips, avg loss ~78-83 pips; judge systems by expectancy, not win rate. Hougaard himself wins ~39% with 48% losers.
- Adding to winners can turn a large open profit into a loss when the market reverses (DAX 11,288 short example, -25 pts on 4,500 DKK/pt); stops for all units must be moved together; total risk rises quickly if stops are not advanced.
- Beware selection bias when drawing trend lines, Fibonacci levels or candlestick names after the fact; academic work finds candlestick patterns have no significant predictive value. Any pattern idea needs systematic, mechanical testing.
- Gaps do not always fill (48% same day); generalizations like "all gaps get filled" are false.
- Daily direction is ~50/50 (Dow 50.4% up days over 30 years), so do not infer intraday direction from higher-TF trend alone.
- Overnight/weekend positions: gap-through-stop and margin close-out risk (Adam story, limit-up open); intraday-only is his way ("I value my sleep too much").
- Overtrading and impulse entries (80% of his early trades were impulse) and carrying a prior loss into the next trade; do not trade to "get back to even" or to offset a previous loss.
- Slump risk: sizing up to a record 400 contracts then losing 50% of month profits in a week; in sizing tests, include correlated-loss clusters and FTMO daily-loss limits (his style of adding aggressively would hit a 5% daily limit easily).
- Author caveats: personal process, "my way, not the way"; track record is self-published Telegram data; no independent audit; many statistics (48% gap fill, 90% of extremes in first 1.5 h, 20% trend days, Friday trend days) are asserted without data -> verify before use.
- Inconsistency in book: add-on spacing "N" vs worked example N/2; loss numbers 78 vs 83 pips; so treat parameters as illustrative.
