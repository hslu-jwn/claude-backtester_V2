# Brent Donnelly, *Alpha Trader* (publisher not identified, 2021)

Short: **AT**. Read 2026-11-18 by Codex; PDF in the library folder Tier 2. The text identifies the 2021 first edition but does not name a publisher.

The book is primarily a guide to discretionary trading, combining market microstructure, narrative, positioning, execution and risk management. Its flow examples are useful for identifying who trades and when, but they generally lack complete mechanical rules and measured returns. The strongest recurring example is an FX month-end hedging effect, which maps to a family already judged. (AT p. 272–275, 287, 345, 405)

## Testable ideas

- **Crude oil settlement imbalance** (AT p. 287): Near the NYMEX crude close, hedging of Trade at Settlement orders can create a large move when volume is imbalanced. The book gives **no entry direction, exit, holding period, imbalance threshold, effect size or sample**. A test would need timestamped crude futures prices and a usable measure of settlement-order imbalance; M1 prices alone would show moves but not establish their cause. Venue: exchange crude futures. **NEW** as a research lead, but not yet a complete rule.

- **FX hedge rebalancing at the London fix** (AT p. 405): When US equities rise substantially during a month, the author says foreign investors sell USD as they rebalance currency hedges toward the 4 p.m. London benchmark; equity declines produce the opposite pressure. He describes a GBPUSD rise into month-end followed by a decline in the first week of the new month, conditional on US equities rising at least 3%; the displayed sample contains **37 months**, but no numerical return size is given. Entry and exit times are not fully specified. Data: equity returns and timestamped FX prices. Venue: FX CFDs. **MAPS TO: month-end / turn-of-month**. (AT p. 312–313, 405)

- **Treasury futures buying into the close** (AT p. 284): The author observes buying into the 3 p.m. New York CBOT close and attributes it to rebalancing and duration extension. He provides no conditioning rule, entry, exit, holding period, effect size or sample. Data: Treasury futures M1 prices and information identifying the relevant rebalancing dates. Venue: exchange Treasury futures. **MAPS TO: Treasury month-end duration**, insofar as the driver is month-end duration extension; the passage does not establish a separate daily effect.

## Method notes

- Study the participants, liquidity, transaction costs and intraday activity of the specific instrument before interpreting a pattern. (AT p. 272–275, 283–288)
- Treat a recurring seasonal pattern cautiously: the author warns that some apparent seasonality is random. (AT p. 402)
- Track performance by strategy and instrument, and examine winning-day frequency alongside average winning and losing days. (AT p. 349–351)
- Set a risk budget and stop distance before calculating position size; include an allowance for execution slippage. (AT p. 376–379)
- Account for spreads and other transaction costs when evaluating a trade. (AT p. 276–279)

## Verdict

The **crude settlement imbalance** is the only identified NEW flow lead, with a plausible hedging mechanism, but the book supplies neither a measured effect size nor an observable imbalance rule. It does not yet meet the brief’s threshold for a research card. The more concrete FX rebalancing example belongs to the already-judged month-end family; its 37-month sample is not an effect-size estimate. The Treasury close observation likewise lacks a measured size and does not establish a distinct effect from the existing duration work. (AT p. 284, 287, 312–313, 405)
