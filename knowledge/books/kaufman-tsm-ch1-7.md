# Kaufman, Trading Systems and Methods (5th ed.), Ch. 1-7 - notes

(Page numbers are the book's own. Everything below is paraphrased. The author tests mostly daily futures/stocks, 2000-2010, usually WITHOUT costs.)

## Overview
- Part covers: intro and research philosophy (ch1), statistics/probability/performance-measure toolkit (ch2), classic charting incl. Dow theory, trendlines, one-day patterns, continuation and reversal formations, price objectives, candles (ch3), automatable chart systems (Dunnigan, Nofri, outside days, pivots, retracements, DeMark Sequential, Lo et al. pattern study, Bulkowski rankings) (ch4), "event-driven" trends that ignore time: swing charts, Livermore, Keltner, Wilder swing index, point-and-figure, N-day breakout, Donchian, Turtles (ch5), regression (linear, polynomial, multivariate, ARIMA, Kalman, regression slope/bands as trading signal, market ranking) (ch6), time-based smoothing: SMA/WMA/triangular/EMA/double smoothing/Hull etc. (ch7).
- Stance: technical analysis must have complete, clear rules. Simple beats complex (Occam). A system needs a sound premise, not a computer scan of everything. Testing is to validate an idea and show robustness across markets/periods, never to "discover" one. Charts are useful mainly as source of objective, codeable events; discretionary pattern reading is acknowledged as hindsight-prone.
- Strong theme: price NOISE (efficiency ratio) decides which style fits: low-noise markets/long timeframes for trend, high-noise/short timeframes for mean reversion. Equity indices are the noisiest and worst for trend following.
- Event-driven methods (swing, P&F, breakout) react instantly to new highs/lows, ignore sideways time, but carry larger per-trade risk than lagged averages. Time-based (MA, regression) are smoother but lag.
- Already implemented in our project (so only summarized): Dunnigan (ch4), N-day breakout / Donchian / Turtle-style (ch5), SMA/EMA/regression-slope style trend (ch6-7), KAMA/ATR stops (later chapters).

## Chapter notes

### Ch 1 - Introduction (pp. 1-24)
- Technical analysis defined as systematic evaluation of price (and volume/OI) for forecasting; must have clear complete rules (p.1). Prices have fat tails, runs longer than normal distribution allows, so trend systems can work (p.7).
- Data frequency vs strategy: smoothing from longer bars makes trends visible; 20-minute bars look erratic. Trend followers should use low-frequency data; short-term traders (mean reversion / fast moves) use higher-frequency (pp.8-9).
- **Noise measurement (pp.10-14)**: Efficiency ratio ER = |P_t - P_(t-n)| / sum |P_i - P_(i-1)| over n bars (0 = pure noise, 1 = straight line). Distinct from volatility. Alternatives: price density = sum of (H-L) over n / (highest high - lowest low); fractal dimension estimate. Author prefers ER.
- Test (2000-Mar 2012, many futures + world index markets, no costs): 20-day average noise vs profit factor of an always-in-market 40-day MA slope system. Low noise correlated with better trend profit factor (scatter, regression line upward). Most trendy: short-term interest rates, then longer rates, USD crosses, energy, metals. Equity indices: highest noise, worst trend results. Corollary: high noise favors mean reversion. Typical ER values: N. America indices about 0.25, Shanghai about 0.37 (p.16).
- Noise rises as markets mature/globalize: N. American index noise rose steadily 1990-2009 (pp.14-16). Markets evolve; system must adapt (p.20).
- **Eight research guidelines (pp.18-19)**: know your premise first (comes from observation, not from scanning); state it simply; assume nothing; test simplest/most important parts first (transparent solutions); watch errors of omission (costs, risk); question GOOD results as hard as bad ones; no shortcuts (verify others' work); start from the end goal.
- Profile of a system (pp.20-23): costs matter most for fast systems; directional (trend) systems have larger slippage than mean reversion; overstating costs is as harmful as understating; too many filters = overfitting or no trades; diversification across strategies on the same market is an alternative to many markets; monitor live vs expected performance.
- Testing is misguided when used for massive scanning; a robust solution across many similar markets never looks as good as a single optimized one (p.22).

### Ch 2 - Basic Concepts and Calculations (pp. 25-77)
- In-sample / out-of-sample (p.27): all testing is overfitting; you get ONE shot at out-of-sample data; if it fails, changing rules after seeing why contaminates it. Suggests alternating 2-year in/out blocks (details in his ch.21).
- Data sufficiency (pp.27-29): sample error about 1/sqrt(N): 4 trades = 50% error, 400 trades = 5%. Slow systems with few trades should be pooled across markets. Data must contain bull, bear and sideways phases; a one-way history makes any long-biased system look good. Structural breaks (euro, company change) may make old data less relevant, but longer still better.
- Averages (pp.30-37): arithmetic, geometric (ratios/growth), harmonic (time-weighted), quadratic, median (preferred to mean for skewed data), mode. Relationship mean > median > mode in skewed data.
- Moments (pp.37-43): variance/stdev (68/95.5/99.7%), skewness, kurtosis. **Kurtosis of daily returns above about 7-8 should make you suspect overfitting** (many same-sized wins) (p.43). Price-level distributions are skewed so stdev bands can fail; percentile (histogram) bands work better (p.43).
- Autocorrelation (pp.43-44): correlate series with itself shifted; Durbin-Watson d (2 = none, <2 positive, >2 negative).
- Probability of a return (pp.44-46), standard error, t-statistic (p.47): t = mean change * sqrt(n) / stdev of changes; degrees of freedom = n trades; more parameters need more trades.
- **2-sample t-test (pp.47-48)**: compare two periods' trade returns to see whether a system performs consistently (use per-trade net returns; Satterthwaite df). Directly usable for our "first half vs second half" stability checks.
- Returns/risk (pp.49-54): standard vs ln returns, NAV compounding, annualize with 252; volatility annualized by sqrt(252); target volatility 12% used for all book results (scale leverage = actual vol / target vol). **Monthly data hides risk**: S&P 1990-2010 vol 18.6% daily vs 15.3% monthly; always use daily equity for drawdown (p.53). Semivariance / drawdown-only risk.
- Performance ratios (pp.58-59): information ratio (return/risk), Sharpe, Treynor, Calmar (return/max DD), Sortino (downside vol). Simplest tend to rank similarly.
- Probability: joint/marginal/contingent, **Markov chain (pp.61-65)**: transition matrix, e.g. P(up | up) = 0.70 and P(up | down) = 0.55 gives long-run P(up) about 0.647. Idea: estimate P(next bar up | n-bar trend) from counts. Bayes theorem for adding event information (p.65).
- Supply/demand, elasticity, cobweb, equilibrium (pp.66-71): equilibrium = lower volatility; imbalance causes volatility. Inflation/dollar can explain long-term commodity trends (wheat example, pp.74-76): hedge out USD before judging seasonal/commodity moves. Ruggiero fundamental rules for interest rates (p.77) - not applicable to us.

### Ch 3 - Charting (pp. 79-149)
- Chart patterns arise from crowd habits: orders cluster at round numbers (even dollars, then 50c, 25c); crowd enters late; "lost motion" overshoot of target up to about 10% (pp.80-81).
- **Dow theory (pp.83-92)**: three trend classes (primary/secondary/minor); bull/bear signalled by breakout of previous rally high / decline low with roughly 10% swings, "20% from extreme" rule of thumb; confirmation between related indices; closing prices only; trend persists until a reversal signal. Bull/bear phases (accumulation, public participation, excess; distribution, panic, no interest). Volume confirms trend (volume not available to us).
- Trendlines, horizontal support/resistance, channels (pp.94-102). Key rules: a bar that pokes through but CLOSES back inside = failed breakout (confirms range); broken resistance becomes support; options for confirming a trendline break: wait N bars, wait for pullback/re-cross, or add a band around the line (pp.97-98). Most big profits come from breakouts that never pull back; waiting for a better entry misses them (p.98). Markets are said to move sideways about 80% of the time (p.99). Channel trading: buy bottom ~20% of channel, sell top ~20%; trade in the trend direction only; break of the channel line = exit and reverse (pp.100-101). Oster (FRBNY 2000) found trading-firm support/resistance levels predicted intraday FX interruptions, valid for about 5 days (p.95 footnote).
- **One-day patterns (pp.102-113)**: gaps (common/breakaway/runaway/exhaustion), spike (high exceeds prior n highs and next bar's high by k*ATR, k>0.75, p.107), island reversal, pivot point (high/low in the middle of n bars; 1-3 bars each side), cups and caps (3-bar pivots with confirmation within 3 days), reversal day, key reversal (outside + new n-day extreme + lower close; code on p.111), 2-bar reversal (Pring), wide-range, inside, outside days. Key facts: studies found simple key-reversal "strikingly unimpressive"; adding a volatility filter (today's true range > 1.5 x 20-day average) improved heating-oil test markedly (p.111). Colby: pivot-point channel breakouts more reliable than trading in the direction of the reversal (p.108); Colby's pivot reverse system on DJIA 101 years about 7000 trades, "significant profits" (p.108). Gap closure within a week (Bulkowski, 100 stocks, p.105; table columns garbled in extraction, the sense is breakaway gaps rarely close, continuation gaps close ~58-72%... verify): common gaps are faded, breakaway gaps traded with the move.
- Common one-day patterns are frequent hence weak alone; combine with other patterns or filters (p.113).
- Continuation patterns: symmetric/ascending/descending triangle, flag, pennant, wedge, run days (pp.114-117). Triangle min about 2 weeks. If triangle apex narrower than normal noise level, breakout just restores normal volatility and is NOT a real breakout (p.118).
- Tops/bottoms (pp.118-132): V-top/bottom, double/triple, extended rectangle bottom with scale-in plan (buy near support, stop on new low, add on pullback to old resistance), rounded, wedge, head-and-shoulders with 3 entry styles (confirmation at neckline break / anticipate right shoulder / anticipate head). Warnings: tops are hard; many "double tops" vanish into a continuing bull move; sell only after confirmation (p.126).
- **Price objectives (pp.133-139)**: all based on pattern volatility. Consolidation: target = breakout level +/- pattern height (or 20% less for conservative, p.134); channel: width measured perpendicular; H&S: head-to-neckline distance projected from neckline break; flag: size of prior move; triangle: size of first reaction; "Rule of Seven" (Sklarew): from initial leg L, up objectives 7/4 L, 7/3 L, 7/2 L; down 7/5, 7/4, 7/3 L (pp.138-139). Use multiple partial targets; trend change overrides target.
- Candlesticks (pp.139-144): definitions, engulfing coded exactly (p.142), morning/evening star (body-size conditions vs 20-day average body gave few S&P signals but good next-day results), Qstick = SMA(8) of (close-open), buy when it crosses above its own 8-bar average, "body momentum" = sum up bodies / (sum up + sum |down| bodies) over 14 bars, >70 whites dominate, <20 blacks dominate; shadow trends; pivot-point levels P=(H+L+C)/3, R1=2P-L, S1=2P-H, R2=P+H-L, S2=P-H+L (p.143). Bulkowski: best candle patterns close within the lower third of the bar, occur in bear markets, long wicks and tall candles outperform (p.144).
- Practical (pp.144-148): longer-term trends are more reliable; trend recognized in 60% of cases is enough because trend systems win only 30-35% of trades but wins are larger (p.144). Failures of patterns are signals (reverse). "Change of character": reactions inside a trend suddenly get larger = exit (p.146). Bull/bear traps: failed breakouts, reverse promptly (p.146). **Replay test**: cover the chart, reveal a bar at a time, record only orders that would have been filled - no cheating (p.147). Equity index arbitrage made all stocks move with the index (p.148); Asian indices nearly identical after vol adjustment (pp.149-150).

### Ch 4 - Charting Systems and Techniques (pp. 151-179)
- **Dunnigan (pp.152-154)**: swing definition by both high and low (upswing continues while highs and lows rise, inside/outside days ignored); five buy patterns (test of bottom within a %, closing-price reversal, narrow range < half largest range of the swing, inside range, penetration of top); need two of first four, then a "thrust" next day. One-Way Formula needs thrust with whole range outside prior day after a double/ascending bottom. Ruggiero's coded "Dunnigan trend": uptrend = two consecutive bars with both higher highs and higher lows, confirmed by break of high of current downtrend. Square-root theory (price moves in square-root steps) is esoteric. Thrust should be measured in ATR/stdev units (p.154). [Already built in our project.]
- **Nofri congestion-phase system (pp.155-157)**: range-trading, third-day reversal. Rules: inside a defined congestion range, after two consecutive closes in the same direction, take the OPPOSITE position at the close of day two, take profit at the next close (day three); if that day also moves against you, you exit with a loss and re-enter (rule still true). Congestion area top = high followed by two lower closes, bottom = low followed by two higher closes; new extreme cancels range; wait 10 days after a "large move" (10-day net change > 2x average 10-day net change), wait 2 days after a false breakout (back inside within 2 days). No stop used. Performed well in wheat for a long time, bad in 2008 crisis; "shortage of non-trend strategies, potential to fill the gap".
- **Outside day with outside close (pp.157-158)**: Arnold's old rule: buy close of an outside day closing above prior high, sell if below prior low, stop at the day's opposite extreme, exit on close 3 days later. Author: worked in the 1970s, not since mid-1980s; **reversing the rules (fade) works much better now**; suggests conditional exit and a volatility filter (skip when the previous range was narrow).
- 3-bar inside-day pattern (Prathap, p.158): up day, inside day, up day -> continuation up (esp. gold, silver, crude); mirror for down.
- **Pivot-point breakout (p.158)**: buy break of previous pivot high, sell break of pivot low, 1-3 bars each side = short-term; 10-20 bars each side makes it look like a macro trend system. Pivots lag by n bars but need no minimum retracement.
- Retracement/proportion ideas (pp.159-167): 100% and 50% most important, then 61.8/38.2 (Fibonacci), 33%, 25%; Tubbs' law of proportion (reaction = 1/2, 2/3, 3/4 of previous move, 4 of 5 claimed); Trident (each wave in the trend equals the previous; enter after 25% of anticipated move, exit 25% before target; trailing stop of 25% of last reaction; advice to martingale up size = ruin risk); DeMark: measure retracement from the highest point since the market last traded at the current low. Caveats: with so many levels price will touch one by chance; confirm with slowing momentum/volatility at the level; allow 10-15% tolerance; enter only after evidence price stopped there. Even-number effect: put orders just beyond round numbers (e.g. 60.25 not 60.00) to ride the cluster of orders; NY Fed confirmed trading activity clusters at round numbers (p.167).
- **Channel breakout by code (pp.167-170)**: either (1) through swing points / regression of the last 3-5 swing lows with parallel line at highest high, or (2) straight-line least-squares fit from last swing, upper/lower band = fit +/- max deviation above/below; project one bar ahead; trend flips when close crosses the projected opposite band (use close, not high/low, to be conservative). Counter-trend: scale into shorts within 15% of channel width of upper band. A minor channel inside a major channel gives faster confirmation. Caution: all trends turn sideways as they reverse.
- Moving channel (pp.170-171): centre M = average of (H+L+C)/3 over n; R = average range; bands M(t)+(M(t)-M(t-1)) +/- f*R/2; long when next price > upper band, short when < lower; profit objective one channel width beyond breakout, moving daily with volatility. CCI (pp.171-172): (typical price - its n-day average) / (0.015 x mean deviation), mean reversion; overbought can last weeks, so simple CCI fade gives many small wins and rare huge losses.
- Wyckoff (pp.172-173): bar chart for direction, point-chart for extent, waves for timing; favors horizontal congestion, ignores triangles/flags as Rorschach tests.
- **DeMark Sequential (pp.173-176)**: buy setup = 9 consecutive closes below the close 4 bars earlier; intersection = a high from bar 8 or later above a low 3+ bars earlier; countdown = 13 bars (not necessarily consecutive) with close < close 2 bars earlier; cancelled if close above setup's highest high, an opposite setup occurs, or a new buy setup (recycle). Entry: countdown-complete close, or close > close 4 bars ago, or close > high 2 bars earlier. Stop: low of the lowest-range bar minus its true range. Takes 21-39 bars to form. Author: only one pattern/one count, robustness can only be shown across unrelated markets.
- **Lo-Mamaysky-Wang pattern study (pp.176-178)**: kernel regression smooths price, then 10 patterns are defined by the last 5 smoothed extrema (e.g. H&S top: E3 > E1,E5; E1 and E5 within 1.5% of their average; E2, E4 within 1.5%; window 38 days; double top extrema > 22 days apart). Tested on hundreds of US stocks 1962-1996; patterns appear far more often than in random walk (control had about 1/3 as many H&S); 9 of 10 had positive 3-day post-pattern returns, 5 statistically significant (H&S top, broadening bottom, rectangle top and bottom, double top). Rising volume generally helped. Details: swing-based codeable.
- **Bulkowski rankings (pp.178-179)**: best bullish: upward breakout of rectangle top, falling wedge, ascending triangle, double bottom, symmetric triangle in a downtrend. Best bearish: descending scallops, symmetric triangle breaking down in downtrend, broadening top breaking down, etc. Symmetric triangle is the most dependable because it forecasts well in either direction.

### Ch 5 - Event-Driven Trends (pp. 181-233)
- Concept: trends as accumulation of reactions to events; systems that ignore time and act only on new price levels (pp.181-182).
- **Swing chart (pp.182-189)**: swing = move of at least a filter size (points, %, or ATR). Algorithm (classic, 2-period): extend swing while new extreme; reverse only when retracement from the swing extreme >= filter. Outside day: keep current direction. Filter as % of price: MSV_t = p x price (use PREVIOUS day's value to avoid lookahead, p.188). Rules: **conservative** = buy when the current upswing's high exceeds the previous upswing's high, sell when downswing low breaks previous downswing low; **active** = trade the first confirmed reversal of filter size (p.188-189). Advantage: no action in sideways drift, signals at the moment of an event, robust; cost: risk per trade can be large (entry vs. reverse level).
- **Livermore system (pp.189-191)**: two filters, a swing filter and a penetration filter = half the swing filter; trade only in the direction of confirmed higher-highs/higher-lows (or the reverse); add on each new penetration; stop at penetration below prior pivot; first penetration against = liquidate, second confirms new trend; re-enter old trend one swing filter beyond a failed reversal point (easier than starting new trend). Exact penetration size never published (maybe ~20% of swing).
- **Keltner minor trend rule (pp.191-192)**: minor trend turns up when price trades above its most recent high of the daily trend and down below most recent low; always reverse. Basis of many breakout systems.
- Pivot points again (p.192): 5-day pivot has a 2-bar lag, no minimum swing needed.
- **Wilder swing index (pp.192-195)**: SI per bar from close-to-close, open-close, prior open-close relative to true range; accumulate to ASI; trade ASI breaking above previous high swing point / below low swing point (HSP/LSP identified two bars later), stop-and-reverse at opposite swing point or trailing 60 ASI points. Limit-move M replaced by arbitrary constant (e.g. 100) since only relative highs/lows matter.
- **Point-and-figure (pp.195-222)**: event-driven chart, box size B and reversal in boxes (traditional 3). Buy when X-column exceeds previous X-column high by one box, sell when O-column falls below previous low. Notes: preference to trend continuation when a bar does both (alternative "reversal first" rule gave earlier stops and tested better unless reversal small, pp.212-213). Studies: Thiel/Davis 1960-69, 799 signals, 53% profitable, $311 average, about 50 days per trade; Zieg/Kaufman 1974, 375 signals, 40% profitable, 12.4 days. Pullback entries: wait for pullback to a point with acceptable risk (stop at the logical level - a nearby arbitrary stop after breakout "has no logical basis"), or enter on second upswing, or require higher-high/higher-low confirmation (pp.207-210). Trade selection: take only if reward/risk > 2 (p.213); 45-degree trendlines as direction filter. Targets: horizontal count (price range base + width x reversal) and vertical count (p.213-217).
- **P&F box size test 2000-2010 (pp.203-206, 218-220)**: 15 markets, fixed % box (0.1%-2.0% of start price), 3-box reversal: very inconsistent; trending (rates, euro) mostly positive, equity indices negative; fixed % box also distorts as volatility changes over time. Variable % box: S&P positive for boxes <1.4%, gold and EUR erratic (Table 5.5). **ATR-based box (50-day ATR x factor) gave far more uniform results and positive for EUR, S&P and gold at most factors** (e.g. factor 0.4-0.8 S&P +21k to +40k, EUR +30k to +55k; gold mixed; Table 5.6) - author's conclusion: volatility-adapted event thresholds are the way, still gross of costs and 3 markets only. Price-volatility relationship is non-linear (crude example).
- Taking profits ("take it and run", pp.211-212): trailing stop = P&F reversal value after a move of 10 boxes; in general taking small profits does not improve return but can improve return/risk; use volatility-stabilized sizing instead.
- **N-day breakout (pp.222-228)**: buy when high exceeds N-day high, sell when low breaks N-day low; conservative = on close. N < 5 is not event-driven anymore. Risk is distance entry to reverse level, grows with N. Donchian 4-week rule (close-based on weekly data). Dynamic N = N_initial x normal vol / current vol (adaptive, p.224). Crude oil test (10-100 days, no costs): only 1 of 19 values lost money, but best N differs by half-decade (75 days in 1991-2000, 15 in 2001-2010, 100 for combined) -> parameter choice unstable; Table 5.7: best N from 10 to 95 across markets, percent of profitable tests ranged 21% (S&P) to 100% (crude, bonds) (p.227). Weekly breakout systems: about 75% of markets showed higher risk than crossovers (Hochheimer 1982). Programming tip: compute on genuine weekly bars, not by guessing "Friday" (holiday weeks). Stridsman dynamic breakout: orders placed in advance from stdev; study time-to-stop distribution (bimodal: quick stop-outs vs long trades).
- **Turtles (pp.229-233)**: S1 = enter 20-day high/low, exit 10-day opposite; skip S1 entry if the previous S1 trade was a winner; S2 = 55/20, no filter. L = 20-day ATR in dollars; stop 2L; unit = 1% equity / (L x point value); add 1 unit per 1 L of profit up to 5 units, stops moved to 2L from latest entry; limits 4/6/10/12 units by correlation; **portfolio rule: cut size by 20% for every 10% drawdown from peak, restore 10% per ~6.7% recovery (p.231)**. Copper 1980-2010: S2 (55/20) steady profits for 30 years, S1 (faster) profitable only in the early 1980s - fast breakouts decayed (pp.232-233).

### Ch 6 - Regression Analysis (pp. 235-278)
- Time series = trend + cycle + seasonal + random. Regression calc period equal to a multiple of a cycle/year removes that cycle (p.237).
- Least squares line, slope b, intercept a, Excel/TradeStation equivalents (pp.238-248). Use sequence numbers 1..n for time, not dates (weekends).
- **Correlation R / R-squared**: R-squared < about 0.20 means regression has no practical value (p.249); detrend series (use returns/differences, not prices) before correlating (p.249). Corn-soybean R=0.91 (fundamental), ABX-gold only 0.26 on short sample.
- Forecast = extension of the straight line; error grows with distance (GE example ch7: stdev of error 1.14 at 1 day, 2.67 at 10 days); confidence bands = fit +/- 1.65 or 1.96 (use 2.0) x stdev of residuals (pp.250-251, 273).
- Polynomial (curvilinear), log, exponential fits (pp.252-258): best statistical fit (curvilinear) gave the worst forecast; the worst-fit log curve gave the most realistic forecast (p.258). Multivariate regression (pp.259-266) for fundamental models (supply/demand, S&P with macro list) - needs fundamentals, not usable with our price-only data. Least-squares sinusoid for seasonal/cycles (p.266).
- **ARIMA (pp.267-272)**: stationarize by differencing, first/second-order AR and MA, correlogram check (no significant lags after differencing gold 20 years, Fig 6.13: first differences have essentially no autocorrelation), estimates less accurate further out. Uses: follow the 1-day-ahead forecast direction; mean reversion at 95% confidence band penetrations, exit at the 50% level, only in the ARIMA trend direction; intraday: buy penetration of forecast high, sell below forecast low, exit on close with stop, trend-direction only (Kepka). Kalman filter combines a base forecast with other timely information (p.272).
- **Regression trading signals (pp.273-276)**:
  - Buy when tomorrow's close > forecast value (n-day regression projected 1 bar); sell when below (Hochheimer/Vaughn). Adding confidence band (forecast +/- 2 x residual stdev) reduces whipsaws. Regression turns faster than an SMA because it compares price to a projected line (Fig 6.15).
  - **Slope rule**: long when slope > 0, short when < 0 (or when slope turns up/down). Slope is competitive with other trend systems (ch8 comparison). Scale: slope in price units per bar.
  - **Add R (correlation of price vs sequence number)** as a trend-quality measure: sign gives direction, magnitude gives consistency; same slope can come from smooth or noisy trend (Tesco "LRS + R-squared").
  - Chande Forecast Oscillator: 5-bar regression, residual % = 100 x (price - forecast)/price, signal = 3-bar average of it crossing zero.
- **Ranking markets (pp.276-278)**: index each market to 100, rolling n-day regression slope of each; strongest vs weakest basis for relative-strength or market-neutral (buy strongest/short weakest or the reverse). Slope ranks change quickly (each stock had its turn leading in a year).

### Ch 7 - Time-Based Trend Calculations (pp. 279-307)
- Forecasting vs following: trend filters assume today's direction is the best guess for tomorrow; lag is the price of ignoring noise. Forecast error grows with horizon, so use 1-day-ahead only (pp.279-283). To be profitable only needs hit rate > 50% with equal moves, or lower hit rate with larger wins. Short calculation periods have no trend (p.283).
- Basic change over time M = p_t - p_(t-n) is the simplest trend measure; with large n it resembles an SMA. Keep asking if a fancier filter beats it (p.284).
- Moving averages (pp.284-293): SMA, drop-off effect (p_t - p_(t-n))/n, average-off, weighted (linear step weights 1..n), group weighting, triangular (weights peak mid-window; double-smoothed SMA is the same idea), Gaussian, pivot-point/end-point MA (weights go negative, reduces lag, can be out of phase), stdev-modified MA, geometric MA (log average; negligible difference on short windows), accumulative and reset-accumulative averages.
- Calculation period choice: avoid periods equal to dominant cycle lengths (they cancel the cycle); if you want to see a cycle, use less than half the cycle length (p.285). 200-day MA is the market's benchmark but too slow for timing.
- **EMA (pp.293-301)**: E_t = E_(t-1) + a(p_t - E_(t-1)); a = 2/(n+1) (Hutson); equivalent-lag EMA is slower than the SMA of the same "days"; 2/(n+1) leaves 13-14% residual weight on old data. Table of a vs days: a=0.10 about 20 days, 0.20 about 10, 0.30 about 6, 0.50 about 3. **When testing, use smoothing constants spaced evenly in a (or logarithmically in days) rather than evenly spaced days or evenly spaced a: equal steps in a put half the tests at 3 days or less; equal steps in days is wasteful at long end** - author says equally spaced constants is the better distribution (pp.299-301).
- Error-corrected EMA (lag correction: add smoothed forecast error back, EE = E + smoothed(p - E)): sits in the middle of the move, more sensitive, "a good candidate for mean reversion" centre line (pp.301, 304-305). Double smoothing weights centre values; Blau's double smoothing of price CHANGES (first 250 days then 5 days etc.) gives smoother trend with one-lag latency (pp.303-304); exponential regularization (p.305); Hull MA = WMA(2 x WMA(p/2) - WMA(p), sqrt(p)) (p.306). Leads/lags of plotted MA (p.307).

## Topic tags

**trend**
- Noise (ER) predicts trend-system success; use longer timeframes for trend (p.10-14). Trend turns are always seen late; 30-35% hit rate is acceptable if winners bigger (p.144). Event-driven trend detection (swing, breakout, P&F) vs lagged (MA, regression): event-driven robust to sideways and instantaneous but larger risk (pp.188, 223). Long-term (policy driven) trends are the most reliable (p.145). Regression slope and R as direction + quality (pp.275-276).

**breakout**
- Swing-high break (conservative swing trade), Keltner minor trend, Donchian, N-day, pivot-point break (pp.188, 192, 222-232, 158). Fast breakouts (20/10) decayed over time while 55/20 stayed profitable on copper (p.232). Best N unstable across periods (pp.225-227). Failed breakout = bar that closes back inside (p.96); breakouts after low volatility vs after high volatility: two camps (p.100).

**mean-reversion**
- Nofri third-day reversal in range (pp.155-157); fade of outside days with outside close (p.158); fade common gaps (p.105); CCI and ARIMA/regression band fades work in direction of larger trend only (pp.171, 271); error-corrected EMA as mean line (p.304). High noise favors mean reversion (p.14).

**momentum/oscillators**
- Candle body momentum (14 bars, thresholds 70/20), Qstick, CCI, Forecast Oscillator, Swing Index, DeMark Sequential counts (pp.142-143, 171, 276, 193, 174).

**patterns**
- Codeable: spike (k x ATR), pivot (1-3 bar), cup/cap, key reversal (+ volatility filter), 2-bar reversal, inside-day triplet, engulfing, morning/evening star, swing-based triangle/H&S/double top definitions (Lo et al.), DeMark Sequential. Evidence: simple key reversal poor alone, improves with range > 1.5x ATR(20); Lo et al. patterns have mild positive 3-day returns on stocks; Bulkowski ranks rectangles, wedges, triangles, double bottoms best.

**intraday/sessions**
- Few direct statements. Gaps arise only with a session close; electronic markets show fast moves instead (p.102). Higher-frequency data suit mean reversion (p.9). Daily close = "evening-up" price (pp.90, 227). Support/resistance lines at intraday FX valid ~5 days (p.95). Round numbers attract orders (pp.81, 167).

**volatility**
- Make every threshold volatility-adaptive: swing filter as % or ATR, P&F box from 50-day ATR (uniform results), spike/key-reversal use ATR multiples, N = N0 x normal vol / current vol (pp.188, 219-220, 107, 111, 224). Volatility compression (inside days, narrow range, triangles) precedes direction change; noise-level caution: a triangle narrower than market noise is not a real compression (p.118). Post-shock pattern: volatility decays back to normal (episodic pattern, p.132).

**stops-and-exits**
- Pattern-based stops (opposite side of the range, below prior pivot, neckline), nearby arbitrary stops after a breakout "have no logical basis" (p.208). Turtle 2L ATR stop and 20/10 exit (p.230). Trailing reversal-sized stop (P&F, Trident 25% of last reaction). Targets from pattern height, partial multiple targets (pp.133-139). Taking small profits rarely improves return but can improve return/risk (p.212).

**position-sizing/risk**
- Equalize volatility across markets (unit = 1% / ATR-dollar), correlated-market caps, **cut size 20% per 10% equity drawdown, restore slowly** (Turtles, p.231). Target volatility 12% annualized as comparison standard (p.53). Martingale-type sizing (Trident) can lead to ruin (p.163). Event-driven systems have higher per-trade risk; size adjusted to volatility fixes largest problem (p.224). Daily-based risk stats needed; monthly hides drawdown (p.53).

**testing-and-robustness/overfitting**
- One-shot out-of-sample (p.27); sample error 1/sqrt(N), need hundreds of trades (p.28); t-tests and 2-sample t-test on trade returns to check consistency across periods (pp.47-48); kurtosis > 7-8 on returns = overfit warning (p.43); test across many similar markets, ignore single best optimized result (p.22); judge by share of profitable parameter values (Table 5.7); question good results (p.19); chart-replay exercise without peeking (p.147); parameter grids should be log-spaced (pp.299-301); a poor statistical fit (log) gave the best forecast, so goodness-of-fit != forecasting ability (p.258).

**costs/execution**
- Trend/directional systems suffer larger slippage than mean-reversion (p.22); do not overstate costs; fast systems most cost-sensitive (p.22). All the author's ch5 test results are gross of costs (e.g. Table 5.3, N-day tests) - so published numbers are optimistic for our spread+swap world. Futures costs negligible vs stocks per his view; for CFD indices/FX this does NOT hold.

**psychology/discipline**
- Take windfall profits if they are life-changing (p.211); simulate bar-by-bar without peeking (p.147); crowd tops/bottoms follow mass psychology (pp.81, 119).

**portfolio/diversification**
- Markets (especially indices) increasingly correlated through index arbitrage (p.148); Asian indices nearly identical after volatility adjustment (p.149-150); diversification can be via several strategies on one market (p.21); regression-slope ranking for relative-strength/market-neutral (pp.276-278).

## Testable ideas

Constraints reminder: price-only M1 -> any timeframe, FX/gold/indices, costs = spread+commission+swap, R-multiples, no volume/OI.

1. ★ **ER (noise) regime filter and style switch** - Compute ER(n) = |close_t - close_(t-n)| / sum|close_i - close_(i-1)| (n = 20 bars; author tried a 20-day average noise; also try 10-60) on each symbol/timeframe. Rules to test: (a) only take our existing trend / breakout entries when ER above a percentile of its own rolling history (or above a fixed level such as 0.25-0.30 on daily; recalibrate per timeframe); (b) the inverse: take mean-reversion entries when ER low. Source: ch1 pp.10-14 (Fig 1.7, Table 1.1). Evidence claimed: positive relation between low noise and profit factor of a 40-day MA trend system across 2000-2012 futures/indices, indices noisiest and worst; noise rose over the last 20 years. Fit: GOOD - cheap to compute from OHLC at any timeframe, usable as a filter across strategies 1-4 and as diagnostic of why they fail on indices; also a rolling regime classifier (no lookahead since uses closed bars). Differs: not a strategy but a gating layer; the failed strategy 4 was run ungated. Use it first to ask "which symbol/timeframe/period is trendy at all" before any new strategy. Also tests whether failure was regime (noise) rather than parameters.

2. ★ **Nofri congestion-phase reversal (range mean reversion), adapted to H1/M30 or daily** - Define range: swing high = high followed by 2 lower closes, swing low = low followed by 2 higher closes; range exists once both are identified; cancelled by a new extreme beyond them; pause for k bars after a "large move" (net change over 10 bars > 2x its historical average) and after a false breakout (back inside within 2 bars). Entry: inside the range, after two consecutive closes in one direction, enter the opposite way at the close of the second; exit at next bar's close if in profit, else hold/re-evaluate under the same rule; exit also when price leaves the range. Optional ATR hard stop (author used none). Parameters: bar timeframe, 2-bar count, 10-bar pause, 2-bar false-breakout. Source: ch4 pp.155-157. Claimed: good for a long period in wheat, damaged in 2008. Fit: GOOD for intraday timeframes on FX/gold: holds 1 bar so swap is avoided if done on M15-H1 inside a session; spread cost matters (many trades), so test net R honestly; on daily it holds overnight (swap, but only ~1 night). Differs: pure mean reversion on a codeable range definition; none of our 4 strategies is range-based mean reversion.

3. ★ **Swing-filter event trend with ATR-based filter (time-free trend)** - Build swing chart with filter F = k x ATR(50) (use previous bar's ATR; author's P&F ATR box k test 0.4-0.8 on daily, 50-day ATR; also try k as % price). Conservative entry: long when current upswing high exceeds previous upswing high (stop-and-reverse on break of previous downswing low), short mirrored; alternative "active" entry at confirmed reversal. Optional Livermore refinement: penetration filter = 0.5 x F, add/re-enter only in confirmed trend direction. Test timeframes M15-D1. Source: ch5 pp.182-191, 218-220. Claimed: swing/P&F ATR-box gave positive gross results on EUR/S&P/gold 2000-2010 over a wide factor range; fixed-% box inconsistent. Fit: MEDIUM-GOOD - event-driven so no lag and no trade when sideways; but holds overnight (swap) in trend; stop-and-reverse risk can be large - convert to R using the entry to opposite-swing distance (or cap with ATR stop). Differs from strategy 4: no averages, no fixed N; threshold scales with volatility; entries require a new swing extreme, not N-bar channel; compare head to head with N-day breakout to see whether the adaptive filter adds anything.

4. ★ **Regression-slope / regression-band trend with R filter** - Rolling OLS of close vs bar index over n bars (n = 20-100, h1/h4/d1). Long when slope > 0 AND R (correlation of price with time) above threshold (e.g. > 0.5, test) ; flat/short when slope < 0 AND R < -thr; or the band version: long if close crosses above forecast (fit projected one bar) + 2 x residual stdev; exit on cross below fit - 2 x stdev (or at the fit). Source: ch6 pp.273-276 (Hochheimer/Vaughn, Tesco, Chande). Claimed: "competitive" with MA/breakout systems in his ch8 comparison; regression turns faster than an MA. Fit: MEDIUM - same nature as strategy 4 (trend following with hold = swap), but the R filter is effectively a trend-QUALITY gate (similar to ER) and could cut the choppy trades that killed strategy 4; treat as an upgrade test, not a new family. Differs: slope/R and residual band instead of SMA + ATR band.

5. ★ **Fade of outside day with outside close, with range filter (short-horizon reversal)** - Pattern: bar range outside previous bar's (high > prev high and low < prev low) AND close beyond prev high (bullish) or prev low (bearish). Author's reversed rule: SELL the close when it closes above the prior high, BUY when it closes below prior low; stop beyond the day's extreme (e.g. high of the outside bar); exit after fixed N bars (original 3; test 1-5) or on conditional exit (e.g. close back past the prior bar's midpoint/hit the opposite side). Filter: outside bar true range > 1.5 x ATR(20) and previous bar not narrow. Source: ch4 pp.157-158 plus ch3 p.111 volatility filter on key reversals. Claimed: original (trend-with) worked until mid-1980s; "reversed rules your results much better" (no numbers); filter said to improve. Fit: GOOD on H1/H4/D1 with R-based stop from the bar extreme; holding 3 bars of H1 avoids swap; test also on daily indices where news spikes occur. Differs: counter-trend event-reversal on one-bar volatility shock, not sweep/zone logic and not a trend follower.

6. **Round-number / pivot-level reaction ("even number" breakout)** - Compute levels at round numbers (gold every 10/50, FX 00/50 pips, indices every 100/500) and daily pivot levels P, R1, S1, R2, S2 (p.143). Test (a) breakout entry placed a small offset beyond the level (e.g. 0.25 ATR) in the direction of breakout and (b) fade at level with stop beyond. Source: ch3 p.81, ch4 p.167, ch3 p.143; NY Fed study of order clustering. Fit: GOOD (pure price, intraday, no swap). Evidence only anecdotal here (order clustering, "free exposure"). Differs: location is a fixed grid, not a swing/zone/FVG.

7. **Bar-count mean-reversion/exhaustion: DeMark-style Sequential/TD setup on H1-D1** - 9-bar setup (9 consecutive closes below close 4 bars earlier), intersection, 13-count countdown (close < close 2 bars earlier), entry options (close of count 13 / close > close 4 bars ago / close > high 2 bars ago), stop = low of widest bar minus its true range. Source: ch4 pp.173-176. Claimed: none quantified; author says robustness only shows across many markets. Fit: MEDIUM (rare signals, need many symbols/timeframes; no swap on faster TF). Differs: counting exhaustion instead of structure.

8. **Spike / key-reversal with volatility filter (2-bar reversal fade)** - Spike: high[1] > highest high of prior n bars and > both neighbours by k x ATR (k > 0.75, n 10-20); trade as a local top: sell on the next bar breaking spike's low (or at confirmation), stop above spike high; mirrored. Key reversal: new n-day high, lower low than previous bar, lower close, with true range > 1.5 x 20-bar average. Source: ch3 pp.106-111. Claimed: filtered key reversal "much better" than raw on heating oil 2005-2011; raw key reversal strikingly unimpressive in older studies. Fit: GOOD intraday on gold and indices (news spikes). Differs from SMC sweep: fixed bar geometry plus ATR-scaled magnitude, no session zone.

9. **Gap fade / gap continuation for index CFDs and weekend/session gaps** - Classify the open gap (previous session close to next open) by size vs ATR and by whether it breaks a recent N-bar range: small "common" gap -> fade toward the previous close, exit if not filled within 1-3 days; large gap through range (breakaway) -> trade with the gap. Source: ch3 pp.103-105 (Bulkowski table: breakaway gaps almost never closed within a week, continuation/exhaustion gaps close often; extraction of the table is garbled so re-check the numbers). Fit: MEDIUM - indices and weekend gaps in FX/gold exist in our M1 data; gap definition depends on broker/histdata session breaks (check). Differs from NY open sweep: uses gap size/location rather than a sweep of an early extreme.

10. **Pivot-point (n-bar fractal) breakout trend, n = 1-5 bars each side** - Long when price breaks above the last confirmed pivot high (high with n lower highs each side, confirmed n bars later), short below last pivot low; stop-and-reverse or stop at opposite pivot. Source: ch4 p.158, ch3 p.108. Claimed: better than swing highs; Colby's pivot-reverse system profitable on 101 years DJIA with about 70 trades/year; larger n resembles macro trend system. Fit: MEDIUM; faster TF reduces swap but raises cost. Differs: no minimum swing size, fractal-based, so complementary to idea 3 (ATR swing filter).

11. **Candle statistics screens (low-cost to run)** - Per-bar features: close location within bar (lowest third closes best per Bulkowski), long wicks, tall bars (> k x ATR), body-momentum (14 bars: sum white bodies/(sum white + |black|), thresholds 70/20), Qstick (SMA8 of close-open crossing its own SMA8). Use as entry confirmation filters for other ideas rather than stand-alone. Source: ch3 pp.142-144. Fit: GOOD, trivial to compute; low expectation stand-alone.

12. **Relative strength / ranking via regression slope** - Index each symbol to 100, rolling n-bar slope (n 20-120, D1/H4); go long the strongest and short the weakest of a related pair/group (GBPUSD vs GBPJPY, NAS100 vs US500, gold vs FX), hold k days, size so risk balanced. Source: ch6 pp.276-278 (and his ch13 market neutral). Fit: MEDIUM-POOR: only 5 symbols, strongly correlated pairs (NAS/US500) and swap on both legs; but legs partly offset swap. Differs: cross-sectional, market-neutral, not directional.

13. **Chart pattern detectors on smoothed swings (Lo et al. style): double top/bottom, rectangle break, H&S** - Use swing points from idea 3's filter instead of kernel regression; rectangles: tops within 0.75% and bottoms within 0.75% of their averages with lowest top > highest bottom; double top: two maxima within 1.5% and more than ~22 bars apart; H&S: middle peak highest, shoulders within 1.5%. Entry on neckline/range break, target = pattern height (reduce 20%), stop other side. Source: ch4 pp.176-178 and ch3 pp.133-138. Claimed: 9 of 10 patterns positive 3-day returns on US stocks 1962-96; 5 significant. Fit: POOR-MEDIUM (stock study, daily horizon, small edge; costs eat 3-day edge). Differs: formal rule-based chart patterns, but still pattern-in-swing logic like SMC.

14. **Drawdown-scaled position sizing for FTMO (risk, not entry)** - Turtle-style: reduce risk per trade 20% for each 10% drawdown from peak equity (for FTMO use tighter steps, e.g. every 2% of the 10% max loss), restore 10% per recovery of about 6.7%; plus volatility-normalized units (risk fixed at k x ATR). Source: ch5 p.231, ch1 p.22. Fit: GOOD for risk/ account layer, runs on R-trade lists; can be simulated without re-running detection.

15. **Statistical-stability toolkit for our own test reports** - Add: sample-error check (trades needed ~400 for 5%), 2-sample Welch t-test between halves/each year of net R (Satterthwaite df), return kurtosis flag (>7), log-spaced parameter grids, % of profitable parameter cells instead of best cell, Markov table P(up | n-bar trend) per symbol as a quick randomness test. Source: ch2 pp.28, 43, 47-48, 61-65; ch5 p.227; ch7 p.299-301. Fit: GOOD, cheap, no new strategy.

(Idea ranking: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 are the ★; 14 and 15 are non-entry tooling worth doing regardless.)

## Warnings / pitfalls
- Tests of the book are mostly gross of costs; for us costs plus swap decide the outcome (p.22 says costs matter most for fast systems; trend systems also have higher slippage).
- Noise is increasing over time and indices are the noisiest market: trend following on NAS100/US500 should be expected to struggle (pp.13-16); our strategy-4 failure is consistent with this.
- Optimal parameter shifts by period (crude N-day: 75 days vs 15 days across two decades, copper 20/10 died after early 1980s); the best single cell is not informative; look at the share of profitable parameter cells (pp.225-233).
- Fixed-size thresholds (points) distort when price/volatility changes; use %, ATR (pp.188, 218-220).
- Event-driven systems appear robust but accept big per-trade risk; sizing by volatility needed (p.224). Weekly/slow breakouts: very high risk per trade.
- Pattern recognition is subjective and mostly seen in hindsight; trends are easiest to see after the fact; rigid codification loses nuance but is the only testable way. Pattern lookahead: spikes, pivots, islands, V-tops are confirmed only after following bars (pp.113, 119, 147) - detectors must emit on confirmation bar.
- Too many retracement levels (50, 61.8, 38.2, 33, 25...) means price will hit one by chance (p.164); needs confirmation (p.166).
- Selling tops/buying bottoms (double/triple tops, H&S anticipation) usually fails when trend continues; wait for confirmation (p.126).
- Simple old rules may have decayed (outside-day trend-follow failed since mid-1980s) (p.158); published systems may be arbitraged away. Martingale sizing (Trident) risks ruin (p.163).
- Kurtosis > 7-8 on daily returns, or unusually good results, should be treated as an error/overfit signal (pp.19, 43).
- Regression forecasts degrade fast with horizon; always use the 1-bar-ahead forecast (p.283); R-squared < 0.2 means the line is meaningless (p.249); correlate returns, not prices (p.249).
- Do not generalize one market's optimum; test across markets and periods that include bull, bear and sideways phases (p.29).
- Book ch.1-7 do not discuss intraday sessions, spreads/swap, or prop-firm rules; everything on those must come from other parts.
