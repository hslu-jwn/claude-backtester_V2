# Kaufman, Trading Systems and Methods 5th ed., chapters 14-20 (book pp. 617-903)

Own-words notes. Page numbers are book pages. "Companion site" = TSM programs named in the book (not available to us).

## Overview
- Covers behavioral/news methods (14), pattern recognition and time/calendar/gap statistics (15), day trading and intraday breakouts (16), adaptive trend and oscillators (17), distribution/zone/Market Profile ideas (18), multiple time frames (19), and volatility, noise, expert systems, fuzzy logic, chaos/entropy, neural nets, genetic algorithms (20).
- Stance: short-term price is mostly noise; news/events cause shocks that are followed by partial reversal; mean reversion has lower slippage than directional entry; costs are the main killer of day trading; every pattern must be verified yourself on current data because patterns decay as people exploit them.
- Most test results quoted are on futures 2000-2011 and are WITHOUT costs (often explicitly "no costs"). Many are single-market, single-parameter illustrations. Treat as hypotheses, not evidence.
- Author repeatedly says: filter by volatility, use higher time frame for direction and lower for timing, prefer rules that work over a wide parameter range (robustness) rather than one optimum.
- Relevant to us: Ch 15 (time-of-day, gap, weekday), Ch 16 (opening range breakout and variants, intraday shock fade, midday S/R), Ch 17 (efficiency ratio, adaptive stops), Ch 18 (daily zones, distribution), Ch 19 (multi time frame), Ch 20 (volatility measures, volatility entry filter, volatility exits). Ch 14 COT/VIX/news/Elliott/Gann/astrology mostly NOT usable (no COT, VIX, calendar data).

## Chapter notes

### Ch 14 - Behavioral Techniques (pp. 617-683)
- News ranking idea (pp. 618-621): price change after a release ~ a*(actual - expected) + b*(revision - previous), a > b; impact fades roughly with the square of elapsed time. Needs economic calendar + consensus data; we have neither.
- Event trading / price shocks (pp. 623-634): after a surprise there is either continuation (underreaction) or reversal (overreaction); lag pattern depends on size of surprise. Detect a shock with a volatility ratio = today's true range / average true range of 60 days lagged by 30 days; ratio > 3 marks a news day (ratio 2.0 used in his tests). Rule by Warwick: buy on event-day close if close is in top 20% of range and the market historically continues; hold N days; use stop.
- Shock reaction results (daily, futures, 10 yrs to 2011): bonds - average shock >1.5pts is followed by slow continuation, but the largest shocks mean-revert for 4-5 days; crude - big shocks (> $4) are followed by decline ~3-4 days; S&P - up shocks >30 pts followed by steady decline (~40 pts over 10 days), down shocks no edge. Memory of a shock "less than 10 days". If the shock day closes near its high/low the next days differ (bonds: upward shock closing low continues lower; downward shock closing near high bounces ~50bp in 2 days).
- Ruggiero (p. 630): 1-day raw stochastic (close-low)/(high-low) on report day; buy if < 0.5, sell if > 0.5, next open, hold 3 days (Treasuries overreact to PPI, retail sales, unemployment). Raschke (p. 630): after a bond jump of >4/32 above prior high, sell stop 3/32 below prior high, stop 1/32 above day high, move to break-even; applicable to FX/indices.
- COT report (pp. 635-641): commercials usually right, small traders wrong; COT index = stochastic of net positions over 1.5-4 years; commercials >80 and small traders <20 -> sell, reversed -> buy; Ruggiero bond timing: commercial index (lagged 1 wk) vs triggers 30/50. Weekly data, futures only. Not available to us.
- Contrary opinion / Bullish Consensus / put-call ratio / Dogs of the Dow / insider block trades (pp. 641-648): sentiment is a filter not a timer; extremes 80-90% bullish. Not testable on price-only data.
- Fibonacci, Elliott, Gann, financial astrology (pp. 648-683): long descriptive material. Only quantifiable items: Elliott Wave Oscillator EWO = SMA5 - SMA35 of (H+L)/2, trend flips when EWO makes new n-period high or rallies a trigger fraction (0.35) off its lowest in n periods (n=40; tested EURUSD & S&P 2000-2011 "excellent" per author, no numbers); enter "wave 3" and "wave 5", exit when EWO < 0. Fischer time-goal days: T = 1.618*(L_i - L_{i-1}) + L_i, entry when close exceeds 5-day high after a goal day; price goals 0.618/1.618 of wave 1. Astrology section is lunar/eclipse formulas. Author says these can only be justified by the performance of systems built on them.

### Ch 15 - Pattern Recognition (pp. 685-735)
- Projected high/low from prior day (pp. 687-688): pivot P=(H+L+C)/3, R1=2P-L, S1=2P-H, R2=P+(R1-S1), S2=P-(R1-S1). DeMark projections bias by whether today's close > open. No performance claims.
- Time of day (pp. 689-699): classic volume U-shape (open > close > midday). Floor traders fade the opening move early; Tubbs hourly stock patterns; Merrill hourly table (strong first hour to 11:00, reversal, weak close).
- S&P futures 30-min time pattern 2000-May 2011 (Fig 15.2-15.6, pp. 691-696): three views (average move, move relative to prior-close-to-first-bar direction, move relative to first bar direction). Average net move near zero; modest continuation in the direction of (prior close -> end of first 30-min bar) through the day; after first bar essentially no further directional drift. By regime: bear 2001-02 continuation; bull 2003-07 average move rises and prices slightly reverse the opening bar direction; 2008 crash all continuation; 2009-11 bull: reversal of opening direction. Filter: with a minimum gap of 50bp the average gap to first bar is 1.2% and the rest of the day continues modestly in gap direction; 200bp gap filter similar. Author's summary: small profit from taking position at end of first bar in direction of the prior-close-to-first-bar move, fails in the strong bull regime; regimes are only known after the fact.
- EURUSD futures (p. 695): net gain all came in the overnight/Europe part; from end of first bar (3am NY) prices tend to reverse; 2000-2011 gain mostly when US session open; recent 2.5 years gain in both European open and US session.
- Highs/lows by time of day (pp. 696-699): the open is the single most likely time of day high or low (about 2x the close, ~8x mid-session); after a large gap (>2%) the gap-side extreme is almost always at open and the other extreme is more likely at the close. EURUSD: three zones (Europe only, overlap, US only); highs/lows cluster at European open, flat during overlap. Trading use: an early extreme becomes more credible as the day goes on; a break of the early high/low after midday raises odds of a close in that direction; buy a midday test of an early low; stop beyond the extreme. In the 1990s bull run the U-shape disappeared (jump at open then straight line).
- Opening gaps (pp. 699-711): tables of how the day closes vs gap size (cont. in gap direction, below open, reversal; crossed prior close; next-open continuation). Findings: for stocks/gold/bonds/EURUSD small gaps (<~0.4%) cross the prior close >80% of the time, closing in gap direction ~45-55% (i.e., no edge by itself); large gaps (>1%) more likely to continue closing in gap direction ~50-65% but also higher reversal. Next-day continuation after gap is ~50% with a few small-sample exceptions. 24h futures (e-mini S&P) have almost no gaps (gap = 3-5% of daily range since 1997); gaps live in the cash-session definition. Overnight gap as % of daily range: pit S&P 20-40%, e-mini 3-5%, EURUSD ~8-12%, JPY 8-26%, falling over years (Table 16.3).
- Reverre gap fade for stocks (p. 707, 710): limit buy if open > 1% below prior close, sell if > 1% above; exit at close. 1% filter small but consistent profits; 2% larger. GE 2000-2011: 1% filter only 2.13 cents/share before costs, 2% filter 3.39 cents/share; mid-2003 to 2008 low-vol period few trades. Close-to-close version: after a 1-day move of +-2-5% fade for 1-2 days; 4% moves lost money 2000-2011, 2-4% worked.
- Weekday patterns (pp. 711-723): encoded each day as X (same direction as Fri-to-Mon move) or O (reverse). Findings: S&P Tuesday reverses 54% ("up Monday, down Tuesday"); gold/EURUSD/bonds show 55-60% reversals in a few branches; most patterns 45-55% with small samples (30 cases at Friday). Adding a trend filter (20/50/200-day MA) raises some branch probabilities by a few points (e.g., EURUSD Tue reverses then Thu reverses 55% -> 61% with 200-day trend). Conclusion: weak, only rely on combos that hold across all three trend filters.
- Weekend patterns (pp. 723-725): S&P and bonds open opposite to prior week direction (Mon open higher only 37% after an up week), but Monday closes with prior-week direction only 47%; so buying a lower Monday open mildly favorable. Combining weekday-pattern with Monday continuation: about half of 16 patterns deviate from 50% (multiple-comparison trap noted by author).
- Day-of-month (pp. 726-729): S&P buying pressure first 1-3 days of the month and last ~4 days; mid-month weak; the end-of-month buying was stronger 2001-2011 for crude, gold, Eurodollar, mixed for others. Suggested strategy: buy ~4 trading days before month end, exit end of first day of month.
- Reversal days (pp. 729-732, Table 15.13): trend-continuation day, reversal day (higher high lower close), outside reversal; next-day open/close odds mostly 40-55%. S&P: after a reversal day the next open often continues but the close reverses so open-to-close rallies (noise/upward bias). Crude similar for down reversals.
- Computer pattern recognition (pp. 732-735): define "good trade" profile (e.g., >= 3% move within 20 days with no loss >0.5%), mark the 5 days before as buy days, search indicator combos that cluster there. Repeated-pattern search: sample size collapses with pattern length; use counts of up days in a window rather than exact sequences. Warning: heuristic search that keeps lengthening patterns is overfitting.

### Ch 16 - Day Trading (pp. 737-777)
- Costs (pp. 738-744): transaction costs are the biggest deterrent. E-mini S&P example: daily range ~$931, fixed cost $10/side, slippage $100 round trip leaves $821; open-to-close range is only about half the full daily range, so capturing a quarter of daily range is the realistic ceiling. Trend (directional) entries pay full slippage; mean-reversion entries need roughly half the slippage (limit orders, fills at the price). Missed orders ("unables") after news jump reduce profits not losses: 10% unables turns profit factor 1.66 -> 1.50 in his example. Slippage model: a0 + a1*volatility + a2*daily volume + a3*time-of-day + a4*order size; at minimum scale slippage proportional to volatility with a floor.
- Trend methods fail below ~3-day holding (p. 745): intraday bars are mostly noise; (1) wait for pullback on directional entries, (2) mean reversion preferred. Moving averages on 5-15m bars: by the time a trend is identified, only ~25% of a day's range remains; trend trades should span multiple days. Bar sizes: avoid uneven last bars of day; equal-tick bars deemphasize quiet midday.
- Key levels (p. 749 + Fig 16.1): traders track yesterday H/L/C, today's O/H/L; volume and volatility fall toward midday; the day's range is tested around midday and a post-midday break is treated as a directional change. Bressert 1970s chart: rules dependent on open vs prior close, today's range vs yesterday's, time of day (buy stop at prior high, sell stop at prior low, tightening as day progresses).
- Overnight gap tables (pp. 751-753): overnight gap share of daily range declining for indices/rates/energy, ~stable for FX (EURUSD 8-11%) -- gap as signal less likely in 24h markets.
- Midday support/resistance (TSM Midday S&R, p. 754): afternoon begins 12:00 local; record morning high, low, highest/lowest close; in afternoon, if flat/short and bar closes in lowest 25% of morning range -> go long; if flat/long and closes in highest 25% -> go short; break of morning low while long -> reverse short; break of morning high while short -> reverse long; hold overnight allowed. S&P 2000-mid-2011: +$268/trade (1 contract, no costs) on 8,404 trades, no parameter tuning. Suited to noisy markets; trending markets maybe afternoon-breakout only.
- Taylor 3-day cycle (pp. 755-758): mean-reverting daily scheme: buy day (low first, test of prior low), sell day, short-sale day; depends on order of H/L in the day; needs daily judgement; book gives example only (soybeans). No stats.
- Opening range breakout (ORB, pp. 759-770): buy when price exceeds open by X, reverse short if it falls X below, exit at close (all day trades). Crabel 1990 % profitable: bonds open+16 ticks 60% (76% after inside day), soybeans 60-76%, cattle 55-73%, S&P ~49-68% (worse on inside days). Updated test by Kaufman on 5-min data to Feb 2011 (Table 16.6): crude Mar2009-Feb2011, breakout 0.05-1.00 $/bbl, no costs, no overnight: all breakout sizes profitable gross; larger breakouts give 50-55% win, profit factor 1.1-1.6, ~$75-95/trade; small breakout sizes give hundreds of trades with ~$5-25/trade (costs would kill them); holding overnight raised profit/trade for small breakouts but lowered profit factor; adding "inside day yesterday" cuts trades by ~90% (25-60 trades over two years) but profit/trade jumps to $700-1,300 and profit factor 1.5-2.6. Gold, bonds (Table 16.7): small profits/contract without inside-day; inside-day filter increases a lot; S&P fixed-point ORB was mostly negative except inside-day cases. He calls profits "too small" for gold/bonds before costs.
- Volatility-based ORB (Table 16.8, p. 764): breakout = k * ATR(n=20 bars, better = bars per day) added to/subtracted from open, k up to 2 (4 possible); similar shape of results, adapts over time. Crude 5m: k=1.3-2.0 no overnight ~13-24k profit factor 1.07-1.18 on 480-740 trades; inside-day panel profit factor ~2.4-3.2 on 60-90 trades.
- Volatility filter for ORB (p. 764): only trade if 6-day historic volatility < 100-day historic volatility (Crabel contraction), halves trade count.
- Raschke NR4 (p. 762): day after the narrowest-range of last 4 days, buy stop 1 tick above NR4 high / sell stop 1 tick below NR4 low, reverse on same-day failure, trailing stop, exit end of day 2 if not profitable.
- First-hour breakout (McNutt, p. 764-766): opening range = first 60 min; entry on break of that high/low; range projection target = average daily range added to today's low after the first hour (and subtracted from high); require 20-pt penetration for S&P; stops/trailing/reversal. Raschke/Connors "Momentum Pinball": setup = previous day's 3-day RSI of 1-day ROC < 30 (buy) or > 70 (sell); buy stop above first-hour high, stop at first-hour low; re-enter after stop; carry profitable trade overnight, exit next open if open is profitable. e-mini S&P 2000-May 2011: +$703/contract on 1,734 trades (no costs).
- Fisher's ORB (pp. 766-770): opening range window 5-30 min (20 for stocks), levels +A/-A at 100% and +C/-C at 150% of OR width beyond ORH/ORL; buy only when price stays above +A for half the OR duration; exit if price falls one tick below ORL; reverse short on staying below -C; exit if not profitable in 1x OR time; stops one tick across opposite side; daily pivot range from prior HLC gives bias. Costly for screen traders.
- Larry Williams ORB filters (p. 770): S&P buy requires %R index < 20% (10-day) and close lower than 7 days ago but today's close > yesterday's, not an inside day; bonds want higher close than 4 days ago; second variant exploiting Mon/Tue strength with entry at open + 20% of yesterday's range. Seasonality filters.
- Afternoon breakouts (p. 770): hold overnight needed because time left is short.
- Rudd intraday set-ups for stocks (pp. 771-772): dip and rally, consolidation, delayed consolidation, early breakout after 15-min narrow range, reversal after false breakout; after a wide-range outside bar closing near its high, buy breakouts of the next day's high after a higher open; sell a fading gap open in first 5 min.
- Fade the opening range, "Intraday High-Low" (p. 772): gap >= 0.5% (S&P pit hours); on designated bar (30-min) buy if gap down / sell short if gap up, stop-and-reverse at the day's low/high at time of entry, exit at close. 30-min bars 2000-May 2011: >1600 trades, +$32/contract average, no costs; author says needs a volatility filter (short/long volatility ratio, or inside/low-vol prior day).
- Gap patterns (pp. 773-774): true gap vs "lapse". Williams: after a true gap up, short on pullback to prior high / prior close; cover at close or next open. Kaufman: with 15-30m data best was entry on pullback to prior close and exit at next open.
- Intraday volume (p. 774): U-shaped; relative volume vs the same bar's average isolates intraday shocks (volume 2x average for that bar).
- Intraday shock fade (pp. 775-777) - "reaction to large bar": shock = bar range >= 3x average of the previous bars (or >= 2x average for same time-of-day bar, 100-day avg). Rules: (open-of-day bar) true range >= 3x prior bar's average and close in top 20% of range -> sell short next bar open, close in bottom 20% -> buy; other bars: same if bar volatility >= 2x its time-of-day average and close in top/bottom 20%; no stop for next 2 bars, then stop beyond extreme of entry bar and the next two bars; exit at day close. Claimed profitable for gold 20-min bars from 2000 and bonds with factor 2.5 (no numbers). Possible extra: exit when volume falls back to normal.

### Ch 17 - Adaptive Techniques (pp. 779-799)
- KAMA (pp. 779-783): efficiency ER = |close_t - close_{t-n}| / sum|close_i - close_{i-1}|; sc = (ER*(fast-slow)+slow)^2 with fast = 2/(2+1), slow = 2/(30+1); KAMA_t = KAMA_{t-1} + sc*(price-KAMA_{t-1}). Defaults n=10 (8-10 recommended, no point beyond ~14), fast 2, slow 30. Trade the slope (buy on turn up). Use a small filter on trend changes (e.g., 0.1 stdev of KAMA changes) so a flat KAMA does not flip on tiny penetrations. This is already tested in our strategy 4.
- Alternatives: Ehlers fractal adaptive MA (FRAMA, fractal dimension), Chande VIDYA (k = stdev(n=9)/stdev(m=30) scaling sc=0.2), correlation r2 adaptive, Ehlers MESA MAMA/FAMA (fast limit 0.5, slow 0.05). Comparison (Table 17.1, daily 1990-2011, no costs, profit factor): KAMA 1.53 avg (best), MAMA 1.36, r2 1.22, VIDYA 1.17; S&P index ~1.1 for KAMA (noisy index: poor), gold 0.86; KAMA also had fewest trades (~160 over 20 years) -> better per-trade; success inversely related to trade count.
- Adaptive momentum: map any 0-100 or -1..1 oscillator into 0-1 smoothing constant; adaptive RSI/stochastic (look-back = slow - ER*(slow-fast)).
- McGinley dynamic: MD = MD_prev + (p-MD_prev)/(0.6*n*(p/MD_prev)^4).
- Parabolic SAR (pp. 790-792): AF 0.02 start, +0.02 per new extreme, max 0.20; SAR never inside last 2 days' range; always in market. Knapp alt: use ER instead of AF: initial stop 6*ATR(10); ER<0.3 keep stop, 0.3-0.6 tighten by 0.1 ATR (long)/0.5? (as printed asymmetric), >0.6 tighten by 0.2 ATR; only advance in favorable direction.
- Mart's Master Trading Formula (pp. 792-793): trend smoothing constant between 0.084 and 0.330 from ranked ATR(15) and net change; band width inversely related; always in market on band break. Fast (5-23 day equivalent) so suited to short-term.
- Trend-adjusted oscillator (p. 794): TAO = midpoint - (MA of oscillator - oscillator) removes sustained overbought drift in trends. Dynamic Momentum Index: RSI period = int(14 / V), V = stdev(5)/avg stdev(10).
- Adaptive intraday breakout (Meyers, pp. 796-797): HR = H_t - L_{t-n}, LR = L_t - H_{t-n}, normalized by ATR(n) and n^a (a=0.75); signals on HRmax/LRmax vs thresholds; QQQ: n=6, a=0.75, thresholds 0.50 and 1.05; flatten 5 min before close; author says about 1 trade/day; four optimized parameters -> overfit risk.
- Variable length MA from volatility (Arrington, p. 797-798): period = (30-5)*((|dP|/sd) - 0.25)/(1.75-0.25) + 5 clipped to [5,30].
- Adaptive process: re-test on recent data regularly; faster reaction means less data; see Ch 21.
- Closing thought (p. 798): adaptive features smooth performance across volatility regimes, but at extreme volatility there are few samples, so judge by whole-sample performance profile.

### Ch 18 - Price Distribution Systems (pp. 801-832)
- Standard deviation basics: +-1 sd 68%, +-2 sd 95%, +-3 sd 99.7%; annualized vol; Bollinger bands (20, 2 sd; compare 21 vs 65 period) lag after volatility spikes; to detect unusual vol compare to a longer, lagged window (e.g., 6-month vol ending 1 month ago).
- Zones from prior day (Jackson, p. 805): Average A=(H+L+C)/3; High1=2A-L; Low1=2A-H; High2=A+H1-L1... six zones; sell short in Zone 4 with stop in Zone 5, target the average; buy near bottom of Zone 3. S&P frequency of closing zone shows slight upward bias. Needs 10-min and daily data. No performance numbers.
- Chande-Kroll forecast zones (p. 807): A = 10-day avg of |daily change|; H1=C+A, H2=C+2A, L1=C-A, L2=C-2A; breakout channel intuition: after a news-driven jump price trades in a similar range at the new level; typical profit target after breakout = previous range.
- Daily-level zones (Scorpio, p. 808): zones defined by today's high + 10-day ATR, high, midpoint, low, low - ATR; study of open-zone vs close-zone transition tables for emini vs day session S&P; open-to-close and close-to-next-open transitions; most 24h e-mini opens fall within yesterday's range.
- Long-term distributions (pp. 811-818): skewness (3*(mean-median)/sd); use median not mean; adjust for currency/inflation; short-term distribution shapes (normal/skewed/bell/opposite-skew) as unstable-vs-stable signals; moving skewness with double exponential smoothing (alpha 0.1, 0.05, 0.333) as leading trend-change signal (qualitative).
- Kurtosis-skew strategy (pp. 819-822): trend version: buy when excess kurtosis crosses below 0, skew > 0, volatility > minimum; sell when kurtosis crosses below 0, skew < 0; mean-reversion version: buy when excess kurtosis crosses above 0 and skew < 0 (volatility > min), sell when crosses above and skew > 0; periods used: 40-bar kurtosis, 15-bar skew, 10-day ATR; stop for longs 5 pts below lowest low of 10 bars, shorts above highest high; only illustrated on e-mini S&P Mar-Apr 2011 (no stats). Suggestions: max volatility, exit when kurtosis flips.
- Market Profile (Steidlmayer, pp. 822-830): TPO (30-min letters) distribution; value area ~70% of activity; normal vs trend vs non-trend days; moves outside previous day's value area are directional; Jones' idea: use 2 sd of TPO count as value area, and a trend filter that blocks MA-trend trades until price leaves the value area. Needs only OHLC/time -> buildable from M1 data (time-at-price replaces volume).
- Distribution breakout (pp. 830-832): histogram of last ~6 months closes, take central 60% (cutoffs at 20th/80th percentile, or 1.5 sd ~86%), breakout when price leaves the central cluster; one example (EuroStoxx 2011) only.

### Ch 19 - Multiple Time Frames (pp. 833-844)
- Principle: trend from a long bar, timing from a short bar. Timing oscillator period must be much shorter than the trend (must signal overbought/oversold about every 5 days if holding period ~1 month, so use < 5-day period). Only take the FIRST oversold signal after a trend turns up. Example: EURUSD 60-day MA with 3-day raw stochastic: trend entry 1.2986 -> exit 1.3152 (+$2,075) vs timed entry 1.2810 -> 1.3306 (+$6,200); later trade timing entered worse. Win rate and slippage improve because entry is against the short move.
- Elder Triple Screen (pp. 835-838): ratio between frames about 5 (weekly / daily / 1-2h; for 10-min chart use 1h / 10m / 2m). Screen 1 trend = slope of weekly MACD histogram (13-week EMA slope); Screen 2 = Force Index (volume*(close change), 2-day EMA) crossing below zero or stochastic below 30 / Elder-Ray (Bull power = high - EMA13, Bear power = low - EMA13: buy when trend up, bear power negative but rising); Screen 3 = buy stop above prior bar/day high. Stop below entry-day or prior-day low, move to break-even quickly, then trail 50% of max profit; take profit when stochastic/Force Index > 70%. Force index needs volume (we lack; tick volume of M1 could substitute? not in histdata - no).
- Krausz (pp. 838-841): three frames with ratio ~5 (10m, 50m, daily); six laws (each frame has own structure, higher frame overrules lower, lower frame respects higher frame S/R, etc.); HiLo activator = MA of highs (buy stop) / MA of lows (sell stop); Gann swings; trade only in the direction of the higher-frame swing; add on lower-frame swing lows. Key insight: a 10-period MA on 1h bars is not the same as a 40-period MA on 15m bars; fewer data points over same span give a smoother series. Also sanity-check: returns per trade vs holding period should form a smooth continuous curve (robust system).
- Pring KST (pp. 842-844): KST = 4*avg9(ROC24)+3*avg9(ROC18)+2*avg6(ROC12)+1*avg6(ROC6) (the 18-period weight is 3 per the author's guess); trade direction from a trendline (about half of longest ROC period), time entry on KST crossing its signal ROC after first turn of trend; stronger moves when all ROCs rise together. Illustrated on S&P weekly 2000-2003; no stats.

### Ch 20 - Advanced Techniques (pp. 845-903)
- Volatility measurement (pp. 845-856): price-volatility relationship is roughly lognormal; use log scale; some markets (FX, rates, energy) are exceptions; price-range measures use more data than returns; five measures of volatility: net change over n days, highest high - lowest low over n, ATR(n), sum of absolute close changes, annualized stdev of returns*sqrt(252); ATR smoother than stdev, gaps handled better; measure over longer interval increases vol sub-linearly; Bookstaber estimators (close-to-close, high-low, HLC combos); relative volatility RV = V(n)/V(m) with m >= 5-10x n, better using non-overlapping lagged window.
- VIX (pp. 855-856, 858-860): implied vol; VIX 25% with SPX 1600 -> 1 sd 21-trading-day move = 115 points; long S&P when VIX high. Connors CVR9 (VIX range expansion reversal, buy S&P on close, exit in 3 days) and 2-day RSI of VIX > 90 with S&P > 200-day MA; MarketSci EMA10 vs SMA10 on VIX. Not usable (no VIX in dataset) unless proxied.
- Intraday volatility and volume (pp. 856-857): intraday volatility shares the U-shape of volume (highest open, lowest mid-session, up toward close, open > close); correlation volatility/volume about 0.6 for NASDAQ-100; meant for options trading.
- Predicting next-day range (Brower, p. 857): higher volatility tomorrow when 3-day average true range is high (very good predictor), when RSI(3) < 40, when prior close is far from open; Mon lowest volatility, Tue/Fri highest (S&P 1987-1995); inside/outside days weak predictors.
- Volatility ATR targets (p. 861): profit target = entry +- k*ATR (k about 3), triggered by intraday high/low; stop = entry -+ k*ATR; with noisy markets trigger by high/low but exit on close; targets for short-term only; in trend systems stops/targets must allow re-entry so as not to miss rare big trends. Bookstaber volatility breakout: buy if next close rises more than k*ATR (k about 3).
- Trade selection by volatility (pp. 861-866): do not enter on very high volatility (high risk) and avoid dead-quiet; decide eliminate vs delay: short-term trading eliminates, long-term trend delay. Test: 40-day MA slope system with 10/60-day trends, 1990-2011, $15 per side, 20-day stdev of returns used as the volatility measure: low-vol-only filter (enter only when vol below threshold of 0.05-0.35 sd units depending on market) increased profit factor and profit/trade in all 5 markets (Eurodollars 2.11 -> 2.50 and +47% per trade; S&P -$37k -> +$6.6k; AMZN PF 1.16 -> 1.51); high-vol-only filter also improved but thresholds inconsistent across markets; info ratio low filter 1.14 vs high filter 0.87 (Eurodollar). Conclusion: entering on low volatility preferable.
- High-volatility exits (pp. 865-866): exit trend trade if |return| > factor x prior 20-day vol (factor 10 for bonds 2000-2011); re-enter only when volatility drops to 1/4 of exit-day return; bonds: baseline +$4,500 (PF 1.02) -> $12,968 (PF 1.07) exit-only -> $34,781 (PF 1.16) with re-entry rule.
- Liquidity (pp. 867-868): countertrend profits assumed at extremes may not be executable (volume thin at extremes); trend entries at extreme prices yield no profit; realistic fills matter.
- Noise (pp. 868-871): about half of moves reverse next day, 25% persist 2 days, 12.5% 3 days; size of moves also geometric. Efficiency ratio (fractal efficiency) measures noise; equity indexes noisiest, short-term rates least; as markets mature, noise rises, trend periods must lengthen; short-term dominated by noise, long-term by trend; mean reversion fits the noisy short term. Match strategy type to ER of market.
- Interest-rate carry (p. 871): rolling futures into a longer expiry extracts carry; not relevant (our CFDs have swap instead).
- Expert systems (pp. 871-875), fuzzy logic (875-880), chaos/fractal/entropy (880-886), neural nets (886-895), genetic algorithms (895-902), hedge fund replication (902-903): summarized below in "Advanced techniques - brief".

#### Advanced techniques - brief
- Expert system = if-then rules + inference (forward chaining); example rules from ADX trending/non-trending (ADX > 18 and rising -> 95% trending), MACD/stochastic signals with assigned confidence; confidences are guesses unless backtested. Fuzzy logic here = combining estimates with weights by reliability (long-term average peak move weighted ~3.5x short-term); fuzzy candlestick detection basically adaptive thresholds.
- Chaos: fractal dimension / efficiency ratio as noise measure (already ER). Conditional entropy: measures how much a pattern reduces uncertainty of next-day move; normalized by log2(n outcomes); basically information-gain screening; sample size caveat.
- Neural networks: nonlinear pattern finder; 70/20/10 split (train/test/final out-of-sample); fewer inputs and layers = more robust; shift targets to smoothed forward indicator (e.g., 5-day avg of 5-period stochastic shifted 5 days) rather than raw price direction; ANN always fits closed systems, market not guaranteed solvable -> must out-of-sample verify; Oscar-winner/NASA launch input example shows spurious inputs get weight zero only with enough data.
- Genetic algorithm: chromosome = rule parameters (indicator type, period, operator, threshold), fitness e.g. profit/trade * (1 - 1/sqrt(#trades)) * (gross profit/gross loss), propagation, mating 10-15% of offspring, mutation ~10%, 70-80% in-sample / rest OOS, run 10 different seedings for global vs local peaks; 4.8e10 combinations of 11 genes searched in ~1,000 passes. Same overfitting warnings as optimization.
- Hedge fund replication: regress fund returns on tradable factors, rebalance on tracking-error threshold. Not relevant.

## Topic tags

### trend
- Intraday trend following needs multi-day holds; below ~3 days trend rules do not work and below ~20 days probably don't (p. 745). Intraday MA trend: after identifying trend only ~25% of the day's range remains.
- KAMA (ER-based adaptive MA) best of four adaptive MAs by profit factor (1.53 avg, PF) with fewest trades (pp. 786-788).
- Noisier markets need slower trend, equity indices noisiest; per-market efficiency ratio can decide trend vs reversion (pp. 868-871, 881).
- Multiple time frame: direction from higher frame (weekly MACD slope, daily swing), timing on lower (pp. 833-841).

### breakout
- Opening range breakout (pp. 759-770): original idea dominant in 1990s, faded after 2000. Best results use volatility-scaled breakout (k*ATR), prior inside day / narrow range filter, only low short-vs-long volatility; all gross of costs.
- NR4 (p. 762), first-hour breakout (p. 764), Fisher OR with time confirmation (p. 767), afternoon breakout after midday range (p. 754, 770).
- Volatility breakout: next close > k*ATR (k about 3) (p. 860). Distribution breakout beyond central 60% of price histogram (p. 831).

### mean-reversion
- Day-trading preference: noise -> fade; mean-reversion entries have about half the slippage (p. 742).
- Gap fades: stock gap > 1-2% fade to close (Reverre), S&P Intraday High-Low gap >= 0.5% stop-and-reverse at day extreme (p. 772), Williams gap pullback to prior high/close (p. 773).
- Fade intraday volatility shock (3x average bar, close in extreme 20%) (p. 776).
- Post-shock daily reaction (ATR ratio > 2-3): mean reversion over 3-10 days in bonds/crude/S&P-up shocks (pp. 631-634).
- Weekday reversal (Tuesday after Monday), weak (54% S&P), month-end/first-of-month flows (pp. 711-729).
- Midday support/resistance reversals (p. 754).

### momentum/oscillators
- Momentum Pinball (3-day RSI of 1-day ROC < 30 / > 70 + first-hour breakout) (p. 766). Williams %R filters (p. 770). Connors VIX/2-day RSI ideas (pp. 858-859). KST (pp. 842-844). Trend-adjusted oscillator and dynamic momentum (pp. 793-796). Oscillator timing must use period << trend period (p. 834).

### patterns
- Hourly/time-of-day patterns, reversal days (p. 729-732), weekday X/O sequences, gap tables, Rudd 5 opening set-ups (p. 771), inside day / NR4, Taylor cycle, Elliott waves via EWO, Fibonacci time/price goals. Pattern length reduces sample (pp. 732-735).

### intraday/sessions
- Volume and volatility U-shape (open > close > midday) stable over 50 years; high or low of day most likely at open, then close (pp. 696-699); open-to-close is only ~50% of the day's range (p. 740).
- First 30-min bar direction vs prior close has modest persistence through the day (S&P 2000-2011) but regime dependent; EURUSD gains concentrated in overnight/Europe, reverses from 3am NY (pp. 691-695).
- Three-zone view for FX: Europe-only, overlap (8-10am NY), US-only; gold/FX extremes cluster at European open (p. 697).
- Gap significance dropped as markets went 24h (pp. 751-753); gap statistics require a defined session open.
- Midday S&R rules (p. 754), afternoon breakout (p. 770), end-of-day exit for all day trades.

### seasonality/time
- Day of month: buying pressure first 1-3 and last ~4 trading days of month; best in index, crude, gold in last decade (pp. 726-729). Weekday Mon/Tue behavior. Mon lowest vol, Tue/Fri highest (p. 858). Holiday exclusion. Williams uses seasonality filters. Event days: US reports at 8:30 ET, 10:00 ET, FOMC 2:15 pm ET (pp. 621, 742).

### volatility
- Volatility ratio (TR / lagged 60-day ATR) > 3 flags shock days (p. 626). Low-volatility entry filter improved a 40-day MA system in all 5 markets (Table 20.2, p. 863). 6-day vol < 100-day vol as ORB filter (p. 764). k*ATR targets/stops (k about 3) (p. 861). Intraday shock = bar 3x average (or 2x same-time bar average). Intraday vol has U-shape. High vol exits with re-entry rule (p. 865). Volatility predictors for tomorrow: high 3-day ATR, RSI3 < 40 (p. 858).

### stops-and-exits
- Day-trade exit at close; Triple Screen stop: below entry/prior day low, move to break-even quickly, then trail to protect 50% of peak profit (p. 838). SAR acceleration (0.02/0.02/0.20). Stop on high/low trigger but exit on close in noisy markets (p. 861). No stop for first 2 bars after a shock entry (p. 776). Time stop: exit if not profitable within 1x opening range time (Fisher) or by close of day 2 (Raschke NR4). Profit targets only for short-term; trend systems should not (p. 861).

### position-sizing/risk
- Little here. Volatility drives risk and sizing (p. 845-846); equalizing risk across markets by volatility. Price shocks are the biggest risk, plan for them (p. 623). Stop-and-reverse always-in-market systems have unlimited-sized overnight gap risk. Holding overnight raised profit per trade but lowered profit factor in ORB tests.

### testing-and-robustness/overfitting
- Want profits over a broad parameter range, not a single optimum (p. 760). Neural net/GA: 70/20/10 splits, fewer inputs = robust, multiple random seeds (pp. 891, 902). Check combos that persist across multiple trend filters (p. 722). Pattern search: sample size falls with sequence length; multiple comparison issue noted (16 weekday patterns, ~9 deviate by chance) (p. 725). Patterns decay as exploited (p. 748). Regimes classified after the fact are not tradable (p. 694). Do not retest ORB size periodically; use volatility-scaled size (p. 764). Always include gross vs net: most results here are no-cost.

### costs/execution
- Slippage at least half a point each way on e-mini; mean reversion has ~half slippage vs trend (pp. 740-742). Unables after news jumps remove only profitable orders (pp. 742-743). Higher volatility makes costs smaller relative to move; choose volatile, liquid markets (p. 738-740). Trade on settle / market on close for exits. Slippage regression formula (p. 743). Volatility-adjusted slippage with floor. $15 per trade side used in volatility filter tests. Small breakout sizes in ORB (many trades) give profit per trade only ~$5-25, which costs would erase.

### psychology/discipline
- Day trading exaggerates bad habits; need discipline and planning (p. 737). Media/sentiment peaks coincide with turns (pp. 622-623, 641). Traders remember strong closes but opens are more often extremes (p. 697).

### portfolio/diversification
- Little: trade many markets/rotate; skipping random trades is fine for high-frequency intraday systems because there are hundreds of trades (p. 762). Correlated markets (bonds/stocks) react in sequence to shocks: rates first, then currencies, then equities (p. 627).

## Testable ideas
All are testable on M1 -> any timeframe, price only. For FX/gold sessions need a defined session open (e.g., London 07:00/08:00 local, NY 08:00 ET, Tokyo); for indices, cash open 9:30 ET (14:30/13:30 UTC with DST handled). Always report gross and net R.

1. ★ **Volatility-scaled Opening Range Breakout (day trade)** - define session open price (cash open for NAS100/US500; London open and NY open for FX/gold); long if price trades > open + k*ATR(n bars) , short if < open - k*ATR (stop-and-reverse once or no reverse; k = 1.0-2.0 tested, n = 20 bars or one day's bars); exit at session end/close; stop at opposite band or R-based. Run on 5m/15m bars. Source: Ch 16 pp. 759-764 (Table 16.8). Evidence: crude 5m 2009-2011 profit factor 1.07-1.18, no costs, 480-740 trades; S&P fixed-point ORB mostly negative. Fit: good for gold/NAS100/US500 (data fits, no overnight so no swap; spread cost matters; use k >= 1 to keep per-trade R above spread). Difference: failed #2 was a sweep-then-reversal (fade) at NY open; this is the plain breakout in the direction of the opening range, no FVG/OB, time-boxed so no swap; failed #4 held overnight with ATR stop.

2. ★ **ORB only after a narrow-range / inside day (NR4/NR7/inside)** - setup: yesterday's range is the narrowest of last 4 (or 7) days, or an inside day; also optional filter 6-day vol < 100-day vol; trade: stop-entry one tick/point beyond today's open +- k*ATR or beyond yesterday's high/low; exit at close of the day (or after day 2 if not profitable, Raschke); stop opposite side of range. Source: Ch 16 pp. 759-764 (Crabel, Raschke NR4, Table 16.6-16.8). Claimed: inside-day filter lifted profit/trade from ~$75 to ~$700-1,300 in crude and profit factor from ~1.2 to ~2-3, but only 25-90 trades in 2 years (10% of days); Crabel 1990 win rates 60-76%. Fit: good for daily-range based filter on gold/indices/FX; trade count small -> need all 5 symbols and 6+ years to get a sample; day-trade version avoids swap. Difference: volatility-contraction conditional, intraday time-boxed; not an SMC liquidity rule.

3. ★ **Intraday volatility-shock fade (large bar, extreme close)** - on 5m-30m bars compute true range of each bar vs average of previous bars (3x) and vs the same time-of-day bar's 100-day average (2x); if bar closes in top 20% (or bottom 20%) of its range -> enter against it on next bar open; no stop for the next 2 bars, then stop beyond the extreme of the shock bar and next two bars; exit at day close (or next session open). Source: Ch 16 pp. 775-777 (and daily version Ch 14 pp. 626-634 where TR/avg TR > 2-3 then fade for 3-10 days). Claimed: profitable on gold (20m bars, 2000 on) and bonds (factor 2.5), no numbers given. Fit: good for XAUUSD and indices on news minutes (FOMC, NFP, CPI at 8:30 ET) using only price; spread widening around news hurts (model spread widening); no swap (intraday). Difference: not a liquidity sweep of a session zone; trades the volatility spike itself, regardless of structure; fade, short holding.

4. ★ **First-bar direction continuation with gap filter (S&P/NAS100 day trade)** - at end of first 30-min bar of cash session compute move from prior close; if |move| >= 0.5% (test 0.25%-2%) go in that direction (or wait for a pullback to a midpoint/VWAP proxy then enter), exit at close; stop at first-bar opposite extreme. Also the opposite fade version (S&P Intraday High-Low: gap >= 0.5%, enter on designated bar, stop-and-reverse at day's high/low at entry time, exit at close). Source: Ch 15 pp. 691-696 and Ch 16 p. 772. Claimed: small continuation edge 2000-2011, reverses in strong bull regime 2009-11; fade version +$32/trade (>1,600 trades, no costs). Fit: only indices with a true cash-session gap; need to define session in M1 CFD data (index CFDs trade nearly 24h with gap at 21-22 UTC). Regime dependence is explicit -> good test of the "stable across halves" requirement. Difference: scored on session-gap structure, not on swing/FVG; both directions testable.

5. **Midday support/resistance + afternoon breakout** - morning range (open to 12:00 local); after 12:00 on 15m/30m bars, if flat/short and bar close in lowest 25% of morning range -> long; flat/long and close in top 25% -> short; long and price breaks below morning low -> reverse short, short and breaks above morning high -> reverse long; exit at session close (or hold overnight as in original). Source: Ch 16 p. 754. Claimed: +$268/trade on 8,404 trades, e-mini S&P 2000-mid-2011, 1 contract, no costs, no tuning. Fit: indices good (noisy); for gold/FX use London/NY lunch. Costs: per-trade value ~ small vs spread, so check net R. Difference: time-of-day conditional range reversion, has reversing at 25% extremes and afternoon breakout in one system; no swap if closed at close.

6. **Low-volatility entry filter on any trend/breakout system** - only act on a signal if recent volatility (20-day stdev of returns, or ATR(20)/ATR(100)) is below a threshold (0.05-0.35 sd units in the book; or relative vol RV = vol(n)/lagged vol(m) < 1); alternatively delay entry until vol drops. Source: Ch 20 pp. 861-866 (Table 20.2). Claimed: improved profit factor and profit/trade in 5 of 5 markets for 40-day MA slope system 1990-2011 (e.g., S&P from -$37k to +$6.6k; Eurodollars PF 2.11 -> 2.50 with 30% fewer trades), incl. $15/side. Fit: easy to add as filter on our failed strategies 1,2,3,4 to test whether net R becomes positive and halves-stable; but the optimum thresholds differed by market (overfit risk) -> test a fixed 1-level filter. Difference: filter not new entry; direct test on existing failures.

7. **High-volatility exit with re-entry rule for trend systems** - exit when |daily return| > factor (e.g., 4-10) x prior 20-day vol of returns; re-enter only after daily range returns to 1/4 of exit-day move or on next trend signal. Source: Ch 20 pp. 865-866 (Table 20.3). Claimed: bonds 2000-2011 net PL 4.5k -> 13k -> 34.8k. Fit: could improve strategy 4 drawdowns and allow shorter ATR stops; swap still problem for held positions. Difference: exit rule rather than entry.

8. **Efficiency-ratio regime switch (trend vs reversion by market noise)** - compute ER (10-20 bars) for each market/timeframe; run trend rule when ER > threshold, mean-reversion/fade rule when ER < threshold; or only use KAMA-style adaptive stops (Knapp: initial stop 6*ATR(10); tighten by 0.1-0.2*ATR per bar by ER band). Source: Ch 17 pp. 781-792, Ch 20 pp. 868-871, 881. Claimed: ER explains why indices unprofitable for trend (noisy) and bonds/rates good. Fit: all timeframes; filter for existing strategy 4 and for scalping systems. Difference: regime classifier rather than direction signal.

9. **Prior-day pivot / Chande-Kroll volatility zones on intraday data** - compute pivot P=(H+L+C)/3, R1=2P-L, S1=2P-H, R2, S2 from prior session (using UTC session boundaries consistent with 17:00 NY roll); daily zones H1=C+A, L1=C-A (A=10-day avg abs change); rules: fade at R1/S1 back to P with stop beyond R2/S2, or Jackson: short top of Zone 4 stop in Zone 5, target average. Source: Ch 15 p. 687, Ch 18 pp. 805-808. Claimed: no stats. Fit: FX/gold highly watched pivots; fine on M5-M15; intraday only. Difference: simple level-based mean reversion, different from SMC structure; uncertain edge.

10. **Time-of-day drift in FX/gold by session (seasonality of intraday returns)** - tabulate average return and sign-persistence for each 30-min or hourly bucket by session (Tokyo / London / overlap / NY), conditional on direction of first bar of that session; trade only buckets/sessions with stable sign across years and halves (e.g., long XAUUSD during a recurring window, exit at fixed time). Source: Ch 15 pp. 689-699 (EURUSD gain concentrated in non-European bars; reversal from 3am NY). Claimed: S&P and EURUSD show structural time effects over 11 years (net gains not uniform). Fit: very good for our data (M1, 24h). Cost per trade: spread -> need average bucket move >> spread; swap avoided if flat at 17:00 NY. Difference: pure time-of-day rule, no price pattern.

11. **Daily shock reaction (daily or H4 TR ratio) mean-reversion for 3-10 days** - ratio = TR_today / ATR(60) lagged 30; if ratio >= 2-3 and close in top/bottom 20% of range, fade (indices: up shocks) or follow (bonds); hold 3-5 days; stop beyond the shock-day extreme. Source: Ch 14 pp. 626-634 (Figures 14.2-14.6). Claimed: S&P up-shock >30 pts -> ~-40 points in 10 days; downward shocks no edge; asymmetry. Fit: NAS100/US500 daily; holds overnight so swap applies (small for 3-5 days vs expected move). Difference: event-conditioned reversion on indices; not a trend following.

12. **Month-end / first-day equity index flow** - long US500/NAS100 from about 4 trading days before month end to close of first trading day of next month. Source: Ch 15 pp. 726-729. Claimed: pattern stable across 1982-2011 for S&P (first 1-3 days + last days), crude and gold in last decade. Fit: indices only; overnight swap on long index CFD (check long-side financing); roughly 5 days/month. Difference: calendar-based, no price signal.

13. **Weekend gap / Monday open conditional** - Monday open vs prior Friday close and prior week direction: after an up week S&P opens lower 63% of time; buy lower Monday open exit close. Source: Ch 15 pp. 723-725. Claimed: Monday closes with prior-week direction 47%. Fit: weak edge, multiple weekday sequences prone to overfit; test as a single rule only.

14. **Elder Triple Screen price-only variant (2 screens)** - Screen 1 daily (or 4h) slope of EMA(13)/MACD histogram; Screen 2 (1h or 15m) short stochastic < 30 (buy) in uptrend, only first oversold after trend turn; entry buy stop above prior bar high; stop below recent low, move to break-even, trail 50% of peak profit. Source: Ch 19 pp. 835-838. Claimed: no stats; example EURUSD 60-day MA + 3-day stochastic gave 3x profit on one trade. Fit: all symbols; if holding overnight swap issue (as in strategy 4). Difference: pullback timing inside higher-timeframe trend, tests our previously failed 'higher-timeframe trend + lower timeframe entry' differently (oscillator timing instead of SMC).

15. **Market Profile style value area filter** - build TPO/time-at-price distribution from M1 for prior session; value area = central 70% of time-at-price; trade breakouts only when price exits prior value area (continue) and fade failed excursions back into value (return to POC). Source: Ch 18 pp. 822-830. Claimed: conceptual; author says no objective test published except Jones overlay. Fit: good, M1 gives accurate time-at-price. Difference: auction-theory structure, different from FVG/OB.

16. **Kurtosis/skew regime strategy** - 40-bar excess kurtosis crossing zero with 15-bar skew sign as direction (trend) or fade (reversion), min ATR; stop 10-bar extreme. Source: Ch 18 pp. 819-822. Claimed: only chart examples. Fit: low evidence; low priority.

## Warnings / pitfalls
- Almost all numbers are gross (no spread/commission/slippage) and on pit/futures data from 2000-2011; ORB's heyday ended around 2000, S&P ORB with fixed points was negative in his tests; gold/bond ORB profits "too small" (pp. 760-762).
- Costs dominate day trading: slippage plus commission can consume the entire per-trade profit for small breakouts; trend entries pay more slippage than mean reversion; unfilled orders after news remove only winners (pp. 738-743).
- Open-to-close is only about half the day's range, so day-trading capture is capped (p. 740).
- Short-term price is mostly noise; classic chart patterns on intraday bars are likely random (p. 745). Intraday trend followers need multi-day holds.
- Statistical patterns (weekday, hourly, day of month) are small effects 52-57%, vary by regime (bear/bull/crash), and decay as exploited; multiple comparisons across 16 weekday branches produce some >60% by chance (pp. 694, 725).
- Regimes are defined after the fact; a pattern working in 3 of 4 regimes may fail exactly in the current one (p. 694).
- Fixed breakout sizes lose meaning as price level and volatility change; use volatility-scaled thresholds and do not re-optimize periodically (p. 764).
- Small sample sizes in tables (3-10 cases at extremes) are unreliable (pp. 703, 735).
- Volatility filters can drop good trades; thresholds optimal per market differed (crude 2.55 vs S&P 3.0 vs Eurodollar 1.6) -> optimization risk; prefer filters that improve all markets at one setting (p. 864).
- 24h markets (FX, index CFDs) have small gaps, so gap rules need session-defined opens (pp. 701-703, 751-753).
- Event/news methods need calendar and consensus data; surprise cannot be predicted, only the reaction can be traded; the first reaction may be the wrong direction (pp. 624-626).
- Neural nets and genetic algorithms find chance relationships; use fewest inputs, out-of-sample 10-30%, multiple seeds; a GA's best solution can be a local peak (pp. 886, 891, 902).
- Intraday volume is not available in our data; any rule using volume (Force Index, volume-based shocks, volume-confirmed exits) needs a replacement (tick count of M1 bars is not in histdata).
- Fibonacci/Elliott/Gann/astrology are interpretive; the author says they can only be validated by system performance; not recommended without a rule-based test.
