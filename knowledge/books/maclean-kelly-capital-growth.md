# MacLean, Thorp and Ziemba, eds., *The Kelly Capital Growth Investment Criterion: Theory and Practice* (World Scientific, 2011)

Short: **KCG**. Read 2026-11-18 by Codex; library folder Tier 4.

This collection examines Kelly sizing: allocating capital to maximize expected logarithmic wealth and long-run growth. It brings together theoretical arguments, criticism, simulations and portfolio applications. Its main practical concern is the tradeoff between growth and the risk of severe losses, especially when expected returns are estimated inaccurately.

## Method notes

- **Treat estimated edge as an uncertain input.** The portfolio-estimation chapter examines errors in expected returns, variances and covariances and their effect on allocation. Its illustrative result makes mean estimates especially consequential; the comparison depends on risk tolerance and the model assumptions. **KCG p.249**, Chapter 18.
- **Compare growth with security along the wealth path.** Useful measures include time to a wealth target and the probability of reaching an upper wealth level before a lower one; terminal return alone does not describe this tradeoff. **KCG p.333**, Chapter 24.
- **Re-estimate at decision points.** The dynamic investment framework updates return-distribution estimates and revises the portfolio when rebalancing; fixed-interval and wealth-limit-triggered rebalancing are alternatives to compare. **KCG pp.261, 264**, Chapter 19.
- **Test fractional sizing over finite horizons.** Chapter 38 simulates full and fractional Kelly across repeated favorable bets and reports that even a positive edge can produce substantial losses over a finite sequence. These are model simulations, not estimates of a market strategy’s future performance. **Chapter 38; printed page unavailable in the supplied text.**
- **Stress the assumptions behind Kelly sizing.** The book’s applications discuss estimation error, transaction costs, leverage and discrete rebalancing as practical limitations. A backtest should include those features before treating a theoretical optimum as executable. **Chapters 18, 38 and 50; precise printed pages unavailable in the supplied text.**
- **Evaluate a portfolio of strategies under changing dependence.** Chapter 50 proposes allocation across strategies with downside protection and scenarios reflecting changing volatility and correlation. This is a portfolio-research method, not evidence that any particular strategy has an edge. **Chapter 50; printed page unavailable in the supplied text.**
