# Final report — lead trading-strategy research review

## Executive conclusion

The strongest genuinely new lead is **post-earnings employee selling at high-stock-compensation firms**: a large
three-day regression differential with a forced-flow mechanism and free SEC inputs. It is only B− evidence,
however: one working-paper result, omitted from the authors' published AER article, with no independent or
post-2020 replication. It therefore begins with a return-blind tag/timing/borrow/power audit. Three other distinct
leads survive behind data gates: the **SEC Rule 201 −10% threshold**, **CRSP fast-track IPO inclusion**, and a
**non-overnight follow-on-offering reversal**. The SEC
identifies the official NYSE/Nasdaq Rule 201 archives it used. Anonymous dated Nasdaq retrieval was verified for a
2020 file, but full completeness and NYSE history remain unverified; fast-track IPO inclusion needs point-in-time
public eligibility reconstruction; and the offering
reversal needs a public post-2015 event/timestamp census because its 2.5% source effect ends in 2014. A genuinely new
international rights-issue family is preserved only as a lower-ranked feasibility audit because its median modeled
spread is negative and the US/Alpaca route is uneconomic. Same-calendar-month stock
seasonality remains the strongest additional historical anomaly, but it requires much longer survivorship-safe
total-return history.

The review also found a critical existing defect: **I-105 is non-executable as frozen**. It observes the official
opening print and claims entry in that same auction, although the opening-auction order must be sent before the
print exists. It must not accumulate forward evidence under that label without a newly frozen executable rule.

The result is deliberately narrow. The project has already done far more strategy discovery than its current
data and forward evidence can support. Most attractive-sounding OHLC, intraday, calendar, reversal, breakout,
lead-lag, and momentum ideas are either already tested, cost-dominated, underpowered, or adjacent to a spent
family. The best immediate action remains the scheduled 2026-12-03 collection of already-frozen forward periods,
not a fresh parameter search.

## What would change the ranking

- A stock-compensation audit showing inconsistent facts, late event timestamps, concentrated unborrowable names,
  or inadequate date-clustered power would park the top new lead before returns are read.
- A Rule 201 audit showing incomplete official files, unreliable symbol/trigger reconciliation, or too few
  independent dates would park/data-block it immediately.
- A public reconstruction that matches CRSP fast-track eligibility would promote one development card; persistent
  disagreement would park the adaptation as a data failure rather than call the return hypothesis falsified.
- A post-2015 follow-on-offering census with a reproducible announcement/pricing clock and adequate date-clustered
  power would justify one frozen reversal test; too few non-overnight events or filing lag would park it.
- A return-blind rights-issue census with at least 50 cases having simultaneous executable stock/right quotes,
  available borrow, known deadlines, and viable corporate-action fees would justify one venue-specific card; the
  current Alpaca-US route and the published sample do not meet that standard.
- Credible 15–20 year point-in-time US-share data with delistings and total returns would promote stock
  seasonality and residual reversal research.
- Per-contract futures curves would reopen commodity carry/roll and spread work.
- A repaired macro calendar could reopen announcement-conditioning research, but not generic clock rules.
- Real forward fills that materially revise costs should update all marginal share and intraday screens.
- A pre-priced classic-ZN/Micro-10Y pull showing I-109 cannot be executed near the assumed 15:00 cost would change
  its live route, not its historical verdict.

Two non-alpha operational findings matter. First, Databento currently offers new teams $125 of historical credit
and exact request-cost preflight; use it only for a narrow I-109 roll/spread/fill audit, because the portal limit is
not a proven zero-overshoot cap. Second, Binance currently advertises zero maker fees on USDC-margined perps, but
Strategy 20 is a frozen USDT-taker rule. A USDC post-only implementation is a separate forward variant, saves only
about 11 bp/week under perfect coverage/fills, and must include missed fills, basis, funding, region eligibility,
and the project's adverse prior maker result. Swiss after-tax scenarios also need review: federal safe-harbour
criteria are cumulative and non-hedging derivatives fail one criterion, although failure is not automatic
professional-trader classification.
