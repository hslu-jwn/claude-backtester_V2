# Negative findings, duplicates, and stop list

## Do not spend a new test on these

- Generic M15/M30/H1 bar patterns, candle shapes, RSI/MA crosses, breakouts, retests, gaps, ORB, session extrema,
  round numbers, or simple time-of-day holds. The project already searched these families broadly and the cost
  wall is decisive.
- A standalone end-of-day stock-reversal portfolio. The modern gross effect is smaller than a plausible single
  round trip and would require two legs.
- A source-matched 52-week-high strategy merely because it is not byte-for-byte identical to the killed 12–1
  momentum rule. It spends another momentum-family look against adverse modern evidence.
- Raw or residual short-term stock reversal on the current 2016+ data. Warm-up leaves too few independent monthly
  portfolios, and modern open replications are negative.
- Traditional equity pairs without a new mechanism. Modern profitability decays and the project has already spent
  relative-value/mean-reversion family attention.
- Crypto quarter-hour trading on M1. The published horizon is 10 seconds and the source does not pay fees.
- Commodity carry/term structure as a “new idea.” It is already FB-237 and remains a data problem.
- Reddit order-book absorption, DOM, or delta claims. Required order-book data are absent and the located author
  did not report a profitable algorithm.
- YouTube RSI, gap-fade, ORB, moving-average, and candlestick systems. They duplicate closed project families and
  supplied no auditable independent evidence.
- Prediction-market probabilities as an underlying-market signal. CPI/FOMC events still provide only about
  8–12 independent dates per year, while direct venue trading needs order-book history, resolution logic, changing
  fees, and infrastructure outside the current project.
- Another auction, ordinary index-review, leveraged-ETF, or ETF creation/redemption alpha. I-71, I-103, I-137,
  and related rows already exhausted these neighbours; daily point-in-time NAV and share-count history are absent.
- Generic Hyperliquid whale or liquidation-cascade strategies. Historical address selection is survivorship-prone
  and the liquidation family is already spent. Only protocol-defined HLP backstop transfers survive as a census.
- Spot-Bitcoin-ETF flow timing. Issuer pages and filings do not supply an auditable point-in-time daily flow archive;
  third-party tables create timestamp and revision risk.
- Maker/rebate harvesting. Queue position and adverse selection are absent from bars, Hyperliquid base maker
  pricing is not automatically a rebate, and the project's prior maker-first test was worse than taker execution.
- Treat Binance's zero-maker USDC-perp promotion as a cost scout, not a new edge or a retroactive improvement to
  Strategy 20. The venue, settlement asset, funding, liquidity, and fill process differ from the frozen USDT-taker
  rule, and perfect assumed fills would save only about 11 bp/week at the observed turnover.
- Same-coin cross-venue funding arbitrage. The proposed Hyperliquid/Binance two-leg rule is already FB-037, which
  the project agreed was not worth another look. A 2026 eight-day, 26-venue simulation found only 8/20 top
  portfolios profitable after modeled costs, 12 forced exits, 19/20 spread reversals, and average Sharpe −7.40.
  Do not relabel a known adverse idea as a new venue mechanism.
- Discounted-DRIP pricing-period pressure. The Australian study's gross long–short result is only `t=2.27`, omits
  costs and borrow, and needs plan-specific pricing periods not available in the current US-share data. It is not the
  same as the project's dividend-payment-flow ideas, but that distinction does not overcome the data and evidence
  failures.

## Park rather than reject

- **Non-overnight follow-on-offering reversal:** peer-reviewed and US-native, but the published sample ends in 2014,
  the accessible publisher text does not expose the final sample count, and the study used proprietary SDC event
  data. Admit only a public, return-blind post-2015 announcement/pricing census before any return test.
- **Renounceable rights-issue arbitrage:** genuinely new, with a peer-reviewed international study and open event
  data, but the reported mean is dominated by extreme outliers while the median modeled spread is negative. The
  published US subset is only 18 cases and Alpaca's voluntary-action fees are prohibitive. Reopen only after a
  return-blind census finds at least 50 synchronized, borrowable, operationally executable cases on an accessible
  international venue.
- **Schedule 13D drift:** free EDGAR data make it feasible, but the 2024 deadline change and negative 2025–2026
  drift evidence require a clean current cohort.
- **Same-calendar-month stock seasonality:** evidence is real enough to preserve, but the project lacks the long,
  survivorship-safe total-return history required for a fair test.
- **Commodity/futures carry and roll:** economically grounded, but expired-contract and curve history—not rule
  design—is the blocker.
- **Weekly FX relative strength:** already specified and structurally distinct; blocked by multi-symbol basket
  integration. It should not be replaced by another single-pair proxy.
- **Closed-end-fund discounts:** public prices exist, but stable historical daily NAV, distributions, leverage,
  tenders, and borrow do not. A price-only proxy is invalid.
- **BTP auction cycle:** mechanism confirmed by recent official evidence, but modern normal-regime economics are
  small and source-matched BTP futures history is missing.
- **Fast-track IPO inclusion and HLP backstop inventory:** preserve only as signal-free data/event audits until
  point-in-time eligibility or sufficient independent transfers are demonstrated.
- **Dividend-month pressure:** evidence is credible enough for a corporate-action coverage audit, but about 120
  local monthly clusters and an unresolved point-in-time ex-date source do not authorize a return look.
- **Cash merger arbitrage:** preserve as a future event-data project; it requires complete announcement terms,
  amendments, outcomes, delisted prices, cash-yield/FX accounting, and failure tails across overlapping deals.
- **Binance monitoring tags:** already FB-041 and still below a comfortable independent-batch count. Preserve only
  an official timestamp/perp-eligibility census; a tag is not a delisting notice, and tag, token-delisting, and
  pair-removal events cannot be pooled casually.
- **IPO lockup expiration:** preserve as a future prospectus-data extension, not a test. The forced supply is real
  and the ledger has no exact duplicate, but US return evidence is old, VC/PE effects differ, contractual dates can
  change, and borrow at the event can dominate the apparent short return.
- **Cash-settled convertible maturity:** preserve as a signal-free EDGAR event census. The forced hedge unwind and
  reported magnitude are attractive, but only 21 historical events isolate unopposed buying; settlement elections,
  option overlays, outstanding principal, moneyness, and 10–80-day averaging windows make outcome-free event
  reconstruction the binding problem.

## Research-process diagnosis

The project is not failing because it lacks enough ideas. It is searching markets where typical gross effects are
1–3 bp while costs consume the same order of magnitude, and it often has only 6–10 years for low-frequency rules.
The productive shift is therefore toward:

1. forced or mandated flows with precise dates/thresholds;
2. cross-sectional breadth with many independent observations;
3. low-turnover effects with large margins;
4. better data and forward evidence rather than more parameter grids; and
5. execution overlays that reduce costs without inventing a new entry signal.

The next research dollar should buy information: a clean event census, untouched forward rows, or a data set that
unlocks a whole family. It should not buy another indicator variant.
